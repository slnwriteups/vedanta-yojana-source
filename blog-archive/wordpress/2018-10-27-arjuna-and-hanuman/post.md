# Arjuna and Hanuman

**नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।**

Arjuna was once on a pilgrimage, touring the land with many saints. After covering various shrines, they reached Rameswaram, South India. Arjuna looked amazed at the remains of the bridge that had survived. He wondered, Why did a great archer like Rama had to rely on creatures like monkeys and bears to build a bridge? Why couldn't he have instead bridged the distance with arrows? None of the pilgrims had an answer.
Then, a small monkey who had been following the company for quite some distance laughed out and replied to his question in a manner that was quite a mockery. He said Rama's army had many powerful heavyweight warriors like Sugreeva, Nala, Neela, Angada, and Hanuman whose weight could not have been withstood by a bridge of arrows. Further, the monkey told him that a bridge of arrows could barely handle his own weight. Arjuna felt that his skills were being questioned and hence put out a challenge to the monkey. He said if the monkey was able to make his bridge of arrows collapse then he would make a pyre and burn himself. And thus he constructed a bridge of arrows from the tip of Rameshwaram till the tip of Sri Lanka. The monkey walked a few paces and jumped on the bridge and soon it collapsed. Arjuna again constructed the second bridge but the same outcome followed. In shame, Arjuna made a pyre and was about to step in when a small boy came by and asked what was going on. Arjuna told the boy everything that had happened. The boy quickly replied saying there was nobody who had seen their contest and asked Arjuna to build another bridge but this time he asked him to do it with all the sincerity in his heart without a sense of pride of his skills. Arjuna did as instructed. The monkey walked on the bridge then started jumping vigorously but nothing happened. The monkey then grew to a humongous height and tried to trample the bridge whole to no avail. The boy showed himself to be none other than Krishna to Arjuna and Rama to Hanuman. This the lord by his many miracles broke the shackles of the greed of Hanuman and Arjuna. Hanuman then took an oath to protect Arjuna by sitting atop Arjuna's chariot.
