**Introduction**

I have chosen the advertisement for a Kit-Kat commercial called the "Dancing babies" initially aired in the Indian market about eight years ago. This ad was so popular that it had over one million views just one week from its launch. The caption of the ad was "Have a break, have a Kit Kat." Over the years, there have been several ads that have been produced with the same slogan. Nestle manufactures Kit-Kat. Nestle has been operating in India since March 1959. Nestle started to manufacture chocolates and confectionery in the year 1990. Nestle India's revenue at the end of 2019 is estimated to be close to $1.8 billion. Nestle Kit-Kat is one of the most successful brands globally, and every year, over 12 billion Nestle Kit-Kat fingers are consumed around the globe.

**About the Advertisement**

The advertisement opens to a scene wherein a professor is taking his students on a hospital tour, and one of the students looks at a baby playroom while opening a Kit-Kat. The student starts to eat his Kit-Kat and slowly starts to imagine a scenario of what the babies might be doing when nobody is keeping an eye on them. The advertisement continues to show the visual effect of the baby creating music and dancing. The student keeps watching the babies as he finishes his Kit-Kat. As he finishes, he notices that the babies are just playing around with some toys and rolling around like how any normal baby would act. The CGI used in the advertisement showed the audience babies dancing.

**Demographics and Target audience**

This ad caters to any general audience of all age groups, but more so specifically to young adults and adults or any working professionals who have had a long workday. The ad is trying to help working professionals and generally anyone who is overworked understand that taking a break from time to time can improve one’s productivity.

**Targeted Cultures and Sub-Cultures**

This ad mainly targets a market wherein the people are generally overworked and always sloshed in work 24/7/365. The ad conveys that taking a break occasionally will help you see life from a perspective that you could not have seen if you always kept your nose in working conditions. Another big area that this ad had to address was catering to an audience from various religious backgrounds and diverse lingual backgrounds. India is a country that is home to over a thousand five hundred languages. This ad was cleverly made not to feature any language and focus on the innocence and atmosphere created by the babies' dancing sequence.

**The Message**

The message being conveyed by the ad is, breaks often offer opportunities that one may miss because of our busy lifestyle. Life often offers opportunities to hear, see, feel, taste, or meet someone new and discover something. A good break can often put things in perspective, help one relax, and get them into the right spirit, mood, and state of mind to face whatever comes next.

The ad tries to grab the audience's attention by introducing an element of comedy by showing the dancing babies as there is no way that it would happen in any real-life scenario. The ad's comical aspect is bound to grab the viewer's attention and put a smile on their face.

**Competitor Products**

The product that competes with the Kit-Kat in the Indian market at around the same selling price is the Dairy Milk by Cadbury’s. Cadbury’s began its manufacturing in India in 1948 and has been one of India's leading chocolate manufacturers to date. The advertising approach taken by Cadbury’s is very different as compared to that of Nestle. The Dairy Milk chocolate is marketed under a Hindi slogan which says “*Kuch Meetha ho jaaye,*” which translates in English to “Why don’t you have something sweet?” There have been multiple ads that have been produced under the same slogan that has catered to a broad audience.

**Conclusion**

Cadbury’s and Nestle have been considered competitors in the Indian market and have monopolized the chocolate and confectionery industry for decades. All things considered, though, the advertising and marketing strategies employed by Cadbury’s have generated more sales in recent times as compared to that of Nestle. The Indian confectionery market is now seeing a broader market that consumes chocolates from age groups 10-60+. Therefore, targeted marketing and ads to a specific age group may affect the sales margin from other age groups. Thus, by targeting a more general audience, Cadbury’s has generated more sales and dominated the Indian market.
