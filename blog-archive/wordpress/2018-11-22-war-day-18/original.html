नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।

 On the eighteenth day, Duryodhana was the last of the Kauravas and the warriors remaining on the Kaurava side were Duryodhana, Shalya, Kritavarma, Kripacharya, and Ashvatthama. While on the Pandava side the five brothers stood strong along with Lord Krishna, Satyaki, Drishtadyumna, Shikandi, Yuyutsu and the five sons of Draupadi the Upapandavas. Even though there were limited warriors on the battlefield, the war raged on fiercely none the less. Yudhisthira and Nakula engaged in a tight fight with the sword and javelin. Finally, Nakula kept up his vow to kill his uncle. In another part of the battlefield, Sahadeva engaged in a tight battle with Shakuni. Shakuni was very cunning and try to create a path of dust and escape Sahadeva’s attack with the sword. His attempt to escape was foiled easily by Sahadeva who zoomed by Shakuni on his stallion,  jumped in front of Shakuni and ended his life by stabbing him. Duryodhana who was of the opinion that he had lost the war decided to go into hiding by jumping into the Brahmasarovara(a lake in the heart of Kurukshetra). He jumped into the lake and sat at the bottom of the lake and assumed the yogic stance on the lake bed. Krishna told the Pandavas that if Duryodhana wasn't killed that day, the war would go on indefinitely till Duryodhana comes out of the lake. To draw out Duryodhana Krishna suggested that the Pandavas instigate him and condemn him in such a way that he would jump out of the lake and engage in war without using his head. All the brothers one by one started to insult Duryodhana. When Duryodhana heard Bheema's voice, it was more than what he could handle. He picturized the death of his ninety-nine brothers in front of his eyes and jumped with his mace in hand. Duryodhana engaged Bheema fiercely in the battle of the maces. As the two started fighting, Balarama returned from the Theertha-Yatra that Krishna had sent him on. Krishna had purposely sent Balarama on the yatra as he was strictly against the war. He found out that the war had perspired in his absence when he had returned to Dwaraka. Without any second to waste Balarama made his way to Kurukshetra where he found that his pupils were engaged in battle. Bheema's blows to Duryodhana were doing no damage thanks to his mother protective blessing. Duryodhana soon started to gain the upper hand over Bheema. Bheema was pinned down by Duryodhana and looked at Krishna in desperation to help as he had done with Jarasandha. Krishna immediately shows him a path by tapping on his thigh. Bheema receives the signal and lands a blow to Duryodhana’s thigh. Duryodhana leaped for pain had struck his invincible body. Bheema then picked up his mace and landed successive blows to his thigh till a point where it seemed as though Duryodhana had died. The Pandavas left along with Krishna and Balarama to celebrate their victory. As the sun set on the eighteenth day, Ashvatthama along with Kritavarma and Kripacharya visited Duryodhana. Duryodhana in his dying breath tells Ashvatthama to kill the Pandavas. That night Ashvatthama sneaks into the Pandava camp and is confronted by Drishtadyumna and Shikandi. Both brother and sister fight Ashvatthama but in the end, both of them are killed mercilessly by Ashvatthama. He then goes on to see five individuals who were asleep in the tent. He goes and slits the throat, of all the five and goes back to Duryodhana and tells him that he succeeded in killing the Pandavas and that he kept up his promise to Duryodhana. As Duryodhana heard this his body’s happiness knew no bounds. But at the same time, he knew that his time had come and passed away.
