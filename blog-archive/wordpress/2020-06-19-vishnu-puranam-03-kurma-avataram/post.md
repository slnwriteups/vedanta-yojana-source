# Vishnu Puranam - 03 - Kurma Avataram

![](https://atomic-temporary-175746789.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/d030a-samudramanthanam-1.jpg)

Today we shall see the story of Kurma Avataram and the kshethra maahatmiyam of Srikurmam.

Once there was a dancer who wanted to impress the goddess, Maha Lakshmi. The dancer performed a very intricate set of dances, and soon the goddess appeared before her and presented the dancer with a garland. Shortly after this, it is said that Sage Durvasa was passing by and saw the lovely garland on the neck of the dancer. The dancer offered the sage the garland and left the scene. It is said that the garland possessed a very fragrant smell that attracted bees. Soon the sage started to get irritated by the buzzing of the bees and was looking to dispose of the garland when Indra arrived atop his mount Airavata. Durvasa presented the garland to Indra. Airavata took the garland but instead of throwing it on Indra, crushed it beneath its legs. Durvasa was immensely angered by this insult and cursed Indra, saying he and the world would lose all their wealth. As the phrasing of the curse was vague, all the wealth bearing substances and creatures such as the Kalpa Vriksha, Kamadhenu, and even Goddess Lakshmi disappeared. Indra never knew a world without getting whatever he desired. Hence to bring back all his wealth, he headed to Vaikuntham to ask Lord Sriman Narayana for advice. The Lord told Indra that to get his wealth back; he must churn the milky ocean until all his wealth bearing substances and creatures returned to him. Thus, arrangements started being made to churn the milky ocean when a situation arose wherein there wasn't enough workforce to churn the ocean alone by the devas. Indra approached the Danavas for help, and in exchange for the help, they could claim whatever extra came out of the churning process.
Thus, the Meru mountain was placed on the milky ocean, Vasuki king of the snakes became the rope, and Lord Sriman Narayana in the form of a turtle placed the Meru on his back to provide support from the bottom and Ajita Avataram on the top of Mount Meru while the churning went on. Many substances came out as a byproduct of the churning. The first product that appeared was the Haalahalam poison, which was consumed by Lord Shiva.
Then in no particular order came the Uchaishravas, Kalpa Vriksha, Kamadhenu, Chandra, and Lakshmi. Finally, after completing the churning process appeared Danavantri with a golden chalice containing Amrutham (The elixir of longevity). Both the Devas as well as the Danavas desired the elixir, and hence the Lord took the form of Mohini and gave the elixir only to the gods. It was during this that Rahu, a demon, disguised himself as a deva and tried to consume the elixir when he was caught in the act by Surya and Chandra. The Lord releases his Chakra and cuts him in such a way that the upper part of the body and lower part of the body became separate entities.

**Details of Kshethram:**

**Moolavar:** Kurmanaatha Perumal

**Vimanam:** Ashtadala Padma Vimanam

**Pushkarani:** Shweta Pushkarani

**Travel:** This kshethram is located in the Srikakulam district of Andhra Pradesh.

**Sthala Puranam:**

During the reign of king Swetha Chakravarthi, this area was referred to as Swetha Giri. Swetha Chakravarthi's wife Vishnu Priya was a devotee of Vishnu. When she was observing a fast on an Ekadashi day, Swetha Chakravarthi approached her intending to make love. When she refused, saying the time was not ideal, the king became adamant. She prayed to Vishnu, who created a stream of water, separating the couple. Swetha Chakravarthi was carried away in the ensuing flood, and Vishnu Priya followed him to the hilly terrains of Swetha Giri. The sage Narada initiated a *upadesam* of the Kurma Narayana mantra and asked the king to pray to Vishnu using it. By the time Vishnu appeared in the form of the Kurma avataram, the king's health had deteriorated. Vishnu then made his Sudarshana Chakra make an impression in the nearby land, forming a lake. Swetha Chakravarthi bathed in the lake and regained his health, after which it was referred to as Swetha Pushkarani. Upon the king's request, Vishnu manifested as the deity of Kurmanatha. According to the *Padma Purana*, Brahma officiated the celestial rituals and consecrated the deity with Gopala Yantra. Vishnu is worshipped as Kurmanatha Swamy or Kurma Narayana, along with his consort Lakshmi, who is referred to as Kurmanayaki.

Later, a tribal king visited the Swetha Pushkarani and was impressed with it. After learning about the story of its origin from Swetha Chakravarthi, the tribal king constructed a tank around the lake and began worshipping the deity regularly. The tribal king used to stay in Sage Sampangi's monastery, which was situated on the Western side of the temple. Upon the king's request, the deity started facing west. The sage Durvasa visited the temple later with his disciples; the event of his arrival was considered significant. Rama's sons Lava and Kusha were said to have worshipped Vishnu as Kurmanatha in Srikurmam. In Dwapara Yuga, Balarama visited the temple and was denied entry by Bhairava, who was serving as the temple's *Kshetrapalaka* (guardian deity). Infuriated, Balarama threw Bhairava away from the temple premises. Kurmanatha came to know this and gave Balarama permission to enter the temple. Balarama, in resentment, cursed that Srikurmam would be the only temple where Vishnu would be worshipped in the form of Kurma Narayana. Legends also say that upon Vishnu's request, Anjaneya agreed to guard the temple.

Over time, this kshethram became misconstrued as a kshethram dedicated to Lord Shiva. Swami Ramanujacharya is said to have reformed this temple during his visit to the kshethram and re-established Vaishnavite practices at this temple. An oddity of this temple is that it has two *dwajasthambams*.
