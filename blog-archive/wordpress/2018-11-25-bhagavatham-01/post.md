# Bhagavatham-01

**नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।**

The story of the Bhagavatha Mahapuranam begins in Indraprastha. Parikshith who was named successor to the throne of the Kuru lineage became a very powerful and respected king. One day Kali Yuga took a human form and confronted Parikshith who was the last of the rulers from the Dwapara Yuga whom Kali had still not infected in thought and action. Parikshith was a very strong personality and Kali failed to poison his mind. Kali then pretended to leave Parikshith and his kingdom but in reality placed it’s essence within Parikshith’s crown so as slowly poison his minds with the negative ideals of changing times.
A few days later Parikshith was out on a hunting expedition and at one point felt extremely thirsty. He looked around and noticed a Rishi who was meditating under a tree. Parikshith at first approached the sage with utmost etiquette and respect but when the sage showed no response, by the influence of Kali in his head saw the freshly shed skin of a snake which he flung upon the sage. The sage turned out to be Shamika Maharishi. The incident of the flinging of the snake skin was noticed by the friend of the son of the Maharishi who tells the son. The son in anger lays a curse upon Parikshith saying he would die in seven days by the bite of a snake as he had used the snakes skin to insult his father. Parikshith wanted a way to spend these last seven days of his life in a meaningful manner. Parikshith handed over the throne to his son Janmejaya and went to the Ashrama of the great Shuka Maharishi who was the son of Maharishi Vyasa. Here at the Ashrama Parikshith narrates the story of what had perspired and asked the great sage to help him make the remaining of his life meaningful. When Parikshith asked this of the great sage, arrangements were made wherein many sages assembled at the Ashrama and Shuka Maharishi narrates Srimad Bhagavatham to Parikshith over seven days.
