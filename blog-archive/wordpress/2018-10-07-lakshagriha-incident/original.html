नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् । 
देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।


After the tournament conducted by Drona and the feats performed by the Pandavas in dethroning Drupada, the fame of the Pandavas started to grow.
The rise in the Pandavas popularity was more than Duryodhana could digest. He with the help of his uncle Shakuni plotted to kill the Pandavas by sending them to a house made of lacquer and setting that house on fire when the Pandavas and their mother Kunti were asleep in that house.
Duryodhana had his minister Purochan design a house made of lacquer and had it painted with special paints so that it would block the smell of the lacquer.
A holy date was foreseen by the astrologers so as to start their journey to Varanavat where the palace was being built. They reached Varanavat where the palace was still being built. Purochan gave the Pandavas a makeshift house until the palace construction was completed. Vidura once decided to pay a visit to the Pandavas while the palace was being constructed. He noticed the lacquer and decided to build a tunnel underneath the palace that led to the forest. The next morning when the Pandavas were to move into the palace, Duryodhana and Shakuni decided to pay a visit to see if the palace was constructed as per plan. They paid their good wishes to the Pandavas and returned to Hastinapur. Before returning to Hastinapur, they told Purochan to set the house on fire the same night and make it seem as though an accident caused the fire claiming the lives of their cousins and aunt.
Vidura in the presence of Shakuni and Duryodhana cleverly gave news of the tunnel construction in a language that was only known only to Yudhishtir and himself knew giving him an indication to use the tunnel and escape while setting fire to the palace themselves. On that night the Pandavas pretended to fall asleep leading Purochan to make arrangements to set the palace on fire. As Purochan was making arrangements unknown to him, a woman with her five drunk and intoxicated children entered the palace. As the Pandavas left the palace, they set fire to it claiming the lives of the woman and her children. Hence a rumor spread that the Pandavas along with their mother had perished in the fire whereas they had escaped into the forest safe and sound.
