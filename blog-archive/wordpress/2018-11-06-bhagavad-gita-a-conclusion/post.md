# Bhagavad Gita - A Conclusion

नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।

Today we will be discussing in particular the details of the Eleventh Chapter of the Bhagavad Gita and then we shall see a formal conclusion to the Gita.

13) Seeing God :-

The eleventh chapter of the Bhagavad Gita can be described as nothing short of a miracle. Arjuna is enabled to see the Lord in his Universal Form (Vishvaroopa Darshanam). The sight of the divine form of the Lord was too much for Arjuna to see as a whole . The Lord enables Arjuna to view his Universal Form by giving a set of divine eyes. Sanjaya who narrates the events of the battlefield to Dhritarashtra describes the vision that unfolded as the Lord had everything embedded within him, pairs of opposites good and evil, beautiful and ugly, west and terrible, pleasant and painful.

This will be seen in the following verses
9,12,13,15-18,38-40,43,44

सञ्जय उवाच |
एवमुक्त्वा ततो राजन्महायोगेश्वरो हरि: |
दर्शयामास पार्थाय परमं रूपमैश्वरम् ||

Sanjay said: O King, having spoken thus, the Supreme Lord Shree Krishna, displayed his divine and opulent form to Arjuna.

दिवि सूर्यसहस्रस्य भवेद्युगपदुत्थिता |
यदि भा: सदृशी सा स्याद्भासस्तस्य महात्मन: ||

If a thousand suns were to blaze forth together in the sky, they would not match the splendour of that great form.

तत्रैकस्थं जगत्कृत्स्नं प्रविभक्तमनेकधा |
अपश्यद्देवदेवस्य शरीरे पाण्डवस्तदा ||

There Arjuna could see the totality of the entire universe established in one place, in that body of the God of gods.

अर्जुन उवाच |
पश्यामि देवांस्तव देव देहे
सर्वांस्तथा भूतविशेषसङ्घान् |
ब्रह्माणमीशं कमलासनस्थ-
मृषींश्च सर्वानुरगांश्च दिव्यान् ||

Arjuna said: O Shree Krishna, I behold within your body all the gods and hosts of different beings. I see Brahma seated on the lotus flower; I see Shiva, all the sages, and the celestial serpents.

अनेकबाहूदरवक्त्रनेत्रं
पश्यामि त्वां सर्वतोऽनन्तरूपम् |
नान्तं न मध्यं न पुनस्तवादिं
पश्यामि विश्वेश्वर विश्वरूप ||

I see your infinite form in every direction, with countless arms, stomachs, faces, and eyes. O Lord of the universe, whose form is the universe itself, I do not see in you any beginning, middle, or end.

किरीटिनं गदिनं चक्रिणं च
तेजोराशिं सर्वतो दीप्तिमन्तम् |
पश्यामि त्वां दुर्निरीक्ष्यं समन्ताद्
दीप्तानलार्कद्युतिमप्रमेयम् ||

I see your form, adorned with a crown, and armed with the club and disc, shining everywhere as the abode of splendour. It is hard to look upon you in the blazing fire of your effulgence, which is radiating like the sun in all directions.

त्वमक्षरं परमं वेदितव्यं
त्वमस्य विश्वस्य परं निधानम् |
त्वमव्यय: शाश्वतधर्मगोप्ता
सनातनस्त्वं पुरुषो मतो मे ||

I recognise you as the supreme imperishable being, the ultimate truth to be known by the scriptures. You are the support of all creation; you are the eternal protector of sanātan dharma (the eternal religion); and you are the everlasting Supreme Divine Personality.

त्वमादिदेव: पुरुष: पुराण-
स्त्वमस्य विश्वस्य परं निधानम् |
वेत्तासि वेद्यं च परं च धाम
त्वया ततं विश्वमनन्तरूप ||

You are the primeval God and the original Divine Personality; you are the sole resting place of this universe. You are both the knower and the object of knowledge; you are the Supreme Abode. O possessor of infinite forms, you alone pervade the entire universe.

वायुर्यमोऽग्निर्वरुण: शशाङ्क:
प्रजापतिस्त्वं प्रपितामहश्च |
नमो नमस्तेऽस्तु सहस्रकृत्व:
पुनश्च भूयोऽपि नमो नमस्ते ||

You are Vāyu (the god of wind), Yamraj (the god of death), Agni (the god of fire), Varuṇ (the god of water), and Chandra (the moon-God). You are the creator Brahma, and the great-grandfather of all beings. I offer my salutations unto you a thousand times, again and yet again!

नम: पुरस्तादथ पृष्ठतस्ते
नमोऽस्तु ते सर्वत एव सर्व |
अनन्तवीर्यामितविक्रमस्त्वं
सर्वं समाप्नोषि ततोऽसि सर्व: ||

O Lord of infinite power, my salutations to you from the front and the rear, indeed from all sides! You possess infinite valour and might and pervade everything, and thus, you are everything.

पितासि लोकस्य चराचरस्य
त्वमस्य पूज्यश्च गुरुर्गरीयान् |
न त्वत्समोऽस्त्यभ्यधिक: कुतोऽन्यो
लोकत्रयेऽप्यप्रतिमप्रभाव ||

You are the father of the entire universe, of all moving and non-moving beings. You are the most deserving of worship and the supreme spiritual master. When there is none equal to you in all the three worlds, then who can possibly be greater than you, O possessor of incomparable power?

तस्मात्प्रणम्य प्रणिधाय कायं
प्रसादये त्वामहमीशमीड्यम् |
पितेव पुत्रस्य सखेव सख्यु:
प्रिय: प्रियायार्हसि देव सोढुम् ||

Therefore, O adorable Lord, bowing deeply and prostrating before you, I implore you for your grace. As a father tolerates his son, a friend forgives his friend, and a lover pardons the beloved, please forgive me for my offences.

14) Conclusion:-

We will now summarise the essence of our learnings from the Gita.

The Yoga as prescribed by the Gita is a progressive multi-step procedure. There are no losses in attempts and failure, as every sincere effort s a gain in itself. It consists of the following:
(1) Controlling the senses and purity in conduct of our routine of life, worship, work, food, sleep so on and so forth.
(2) Unselfish performance of one’s duties.
(3) Cultivation of detachment and develop an even spirit in the face of success, difficulty, failure, causes of joy or grief or disappointment.
(4) Vigilantly control the mind by eliminating the the elements of lust, anger and greed.
(5) Surrender to God’s Grace

Each of these aspects are emphasised and given separate names but they are all mutually connected and inseparable and so are all connected in the Gita.
