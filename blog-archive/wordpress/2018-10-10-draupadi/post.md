# Draupadi

**नारायणं** **नमस्कृत्य** **नरं** **चैव** **नरोत्तमम्** **।**

**देवीं** **सरस्वतीं** **व्यासं** **ततो** **जयमुदीरयेत्** **।।**

Draupada after facing humiliation at the hands of Drona wanted to take revenge. To carry out his intentions, he performed a ***Putrakameshti Yagna*** to obtain a son who was capable of killing Drona. From the sacrificial flames emerged a young man, Dhrishtadyumna who would be Drona’s end and also from those flames came a beautiful young woman named Draupadi who Drupada intended to marry off to Arjuna. But when the news came out that the Pandavas had died in the palace at Varanvrat Drupada decided to hold a contest to find the best suitor and a capable warrior to marry Draupadi. Many glorious warriors appeared to take part

in the competition. Duryodhana, Dushasana, Vikarna, Yuyutsu, Karna, Jarasandha, Shishupala to name a few were among the participants. The rules of the competition were simple. In front of all the royal princes was a ***Matsyayantra*** which had a rotating fish which was kept at height. The prince whomsoever could pierce the eye of the rotating fish by looking at its reflection in an oil platform below the platform on which they stand to string the bow and shoot the arrow would be declared the winner. Drupada had specifically designed this challenge keeping Arjuna in mind as he firmly believed that Arjuna had no equal and he was the only archer capable of pulling of such a feat.

Among the distinguished guests invited to witness this grand spectacle were Krishna and Balarama.

In the meanwhile, the Pandavas had made their way towards Panchala where they met Vyasa who told them that good fortune awaited their arrival at the royal palace. The Pandavas continued to remain in their guise as Brahmins and entered the royal court where the function was being held.

One-by-one all the princes tried to lift the mighty bow the ***Shivadanush*** as gifted to Drupada by Lord Shiva specifically to be used in this contest but failed miserably. Once Krishna saw the five Brahmins enter the room, he immediately recognized them to be the Pandavas and passed the hint to Draupadi. As the contest progressed Karna went to on trying his hand at trying to marry Draupadi. Karna effortlessly lifted the bow and strung it. As he was about to mount his arrow to shoot the fish Draupadi raised her voice saying she did not wish to marry a son of a charioteer (***Soothaputra)***. Soon there were no Kshatriyas left among the competitors and hence an invitation was given to Brahmins present in the gathering. When the invitation was given Krishna gazed into Arjuna’s eyes hinting him to try. All the gathered brahmins unanimously came to decision and electively sent Arjuna to string the bow and try to win Draupadi. The gathered princes rose in a fury and started to rebel saying the entire contest was just a plot to humiliate all the kings and princes gathered at the function some left while some retaliated and started to raise their weapons. At this Krishna and Balarama interfered and asked the Brahmin to fearlessly approach the bow and try his luck. Arjuna paid his respects to the bow, effortlessly lifted it, strung it and on his first attempt pierced the eye of the fish. No sooner than he pierced the eye of the fish Duryodhana recognized that the archer was none other than Arjuna and that the other four men who had entered the court along with him were the Pandavas and that they had somehow survived the burning of the palace. Duryodhana in a fury encouraged all the gathered warriors to engage in war against Arjuna. Krishna and Balarama averted any bloodshed and allowed the safe passage of Arjuna, his brothers and his new married wife to their hermitage.
