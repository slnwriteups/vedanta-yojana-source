# An Introduction to the Bhagavad Gita

**नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।**

All the arrangements were made on both sides to fight a war. Kurukshetra was chosen as the battleground where the battle would take place. There were a total of eighteen akshauhini armies that took their footing on the battlefield. Seven of these were allies to the Pandavas and eleven were allied to the Kauravas. The break-up of the same was as follows:
Kaurava Army-
Bhagdatta- 1
Shalya- 1
Karna- 1
Krittavarma(Krishna's Narayani Sena)-1
Jayadratha-1
Sudhakshina-1
Bahlika-1
Shakuni-1
Susharma-1
Kalinga-1
Kuru and other allies- 1

Pandava Army-
Satyaki-1
Kuntibhoja-1
Drishtaketu-1
Sahadeva(Son of Jarasandha)-1
Drupada-1
Virata-1
Gatothkacha-1

The armies were face-to-face and ready to battle when Arjuna looked at all his family and his teachers on the opposite side. Arjuna was overcome by emotion. He thought to himself and dropped his weapons saying he would not take any further part in the war. To remind Arjuna of his true purpose, Krishna narrates the Bhagavad Gita. The Bhagavad Gita is a very important scripture in the fabric of *Sanathana-Dharma*. It establishes the basis of the Vedanta School of thought.
Arjuna engages in a dialogue with Shri Krishna as a guide to understand the rationale behind the war and then goes far deep beyond the barriers of war into the boundaries of human philosophy, ethics and life choices. The Lord in twelve cantos explains to Arjuna who we are, how we should live our lives, and how should we act in the world.

The Bhagavad Gita is a very detailed scripture in itself. I will over the course of the next week explain the essence of the Gita taking up 14 topics
1) The Soul
2) Karma
3) God and Nature
4) Right Action
5) Control of the Mind
6) Meditation
7) Inclination
8) Hope
9) Godlessness
10) Ideals
11) Surrender
12) Advaita and Visishta Advaita Disciplines
13) Seeing God
14) Conclusion
