All the arrangements were made on both sides to fight a war. Kurukshetra was chosen as the battleground where the battle would take place. There were a total of eighteen akshauhini armies that took their footing on the battlefield. Seven of these were allies to the Pandavas and eleven were allied to the Kauravas. The armies were face-to-face and ready to battle when Arjuna looked at all his family and his teachers on the opposite side. Arjuna was overcome by emotion. He thought to himself and dropped his weapons saying he would not take any further part in the war. To remind Arjuna of his true purpose, Krishna narrates the Bhagavad Gita. The Bhagavad Gita is a very important scripture in the fabric of Sanathana-Dharma. It establishes the basis of the Vedanta School of thought. 
Arjuna engages in a dialogue with Shri Krishna as a guide to understand the rationale behind the war and then goes far deep beyond the barriers of war into the boundaries of human philosophy, ethics and life choices. The Lord in twelve cantos explains to Arjuna who we are, how we should live our lives, and how should we act in the world.
