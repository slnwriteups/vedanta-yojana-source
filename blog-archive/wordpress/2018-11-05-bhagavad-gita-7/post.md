# Bhagavad Gita-7

**नारायणं** **नमस्कृत्य** **नरं** **चैव** **नरोत्तमम्** **।**

**देवीं** **सरस्वतीं** **व्यासं** **ततो** **जयमुदीरयेत्** **।।**

Today we shall understand the concepts **“Advaita” and “Visishtadvaita”** and how both these philosophies propitiate to the Lord.

***Advaita Philosophy: -***

This is the oldest school of Vedanta, and it states that Brahman is the only reality and the world is illusory (Maya). Ignorance of the reality is what causes suffering, and liberation can be obtained only by true knowledge of Brahman. It states that both the individual self (Atman) and Brahman are the same, and knowing this difference causes liberation.

Brahman (the absolute) alone is real; this world is unreal, and the jiva or the individual soul is non-different from Brahman.

The Jiva or the individual soul identifies itself with the body-mind complex due to Avidya (ignorance). Its individuality lasts only as long as it identifies itself with its limiting adjuncts. The moment the Jivatma understands its infinite power by jnana (knowledge), it loses its individuality and realizes its Satchitananda nature (infinite nature).

Almighty alone truly exists and all others, seen in the world are not truly existing, but look like existing, which illusory perception goes away, by the true knowledge of Almighty.

***Visishtadvaita: -***

Vishishtadvaita literally means the Unique Advaita, that is, Advaita with some amendments. While it accepts Brahman as the unified whole, it states He is characterized by multiple forms.

Souls are intrinsically the same and all souls are alike in their quality. God stands for the whole universe and matter and souls form His body, He being THEIR soul. God is viewed as the cause and also as the effect.

There is only one Almighty which, is the instrumental Cause, material Cause and assisting Cause in the manifestation, maintenance, and disappearance of the universe and its constituents, only to repeat the cycle again and again eternally. Thus, all others, whether scient souls or inert matter, other than Almighty, are like the body of Almighty itself so that all are inseparably integrated.

Another concept that is key to the Visishtadvaita Philosophy is that on Sharanagti or Surrender to the almighty. There is only one true Lord and he is Sriman Narayana. How much ever we may propitiate to other deities, in the end, the only one Lord that remains to receive those prayers is Lord Sriman Narayana.
