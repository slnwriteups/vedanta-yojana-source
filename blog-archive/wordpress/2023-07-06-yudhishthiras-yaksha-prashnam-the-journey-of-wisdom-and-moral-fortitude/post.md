# Yudhishthira's Yaksha Prashnam: The Journey of Wisdom and Moral Fortitude

Yudhishthira, the eldest of the Pandavas, found himself in the midst of a great trial. Along with his brothers and their wife Draupadi, they were nearing the end of their thirteen-year exile in the forest. As the thirteenth and final year, spent incognito, approached, a Brahmin approached them, seeking their help.

The Brahmin, who was a Nitya-Agnihotri, explained that he had lost his sticks for kindling the fire required for his daily Yajna. These sticks had gotten entangled in the horns of a fleeing stag, and he requested the Pandavas' assistance in retrieving them. Recognizing the importance of upholding their dharma and aiding those who practiced their daily rituals, the Pandavas agreed to pursue the stag.

The chase was taxing, and the Pandavas grew exhausted, hungry, and thirsty. Seeking respite, they rested beneath a tree. Always mindful of his brothers' well-being, Yudhishthira instructed Nakula to search for water. Nakula discovered the presence of water cranes and located a crystal-clear lake nearby.

As Nakula approached the lake, he heard a voice of warning, cautioning him not to touch the water without answering specific questions. Ignoring the warning, Nakula drank the water and fell lifeless to the ground. Sahadeva followed suit, meeting the same fate upon disregarding the voice's caution.

Arjuna, known for his intelligence and keen perception, arrived at the lake next. He challenged the voice of warning, demanding that the voice reveal itself. He did not drink the water immediately but engaged in conversation with the unseen entity. But when the voice did not respond, and he proceeded to drink the water, he too met the same fate.

Finally, Bheema arrived, met with the same warning, and, like his brothers, paid no heed to it, resulting in his demise. Yudhishthira, deeply disturbed by the sight of his lifeless brothers, approached the lake himself, determined to uncover the cause of their deaths.

At this moment, the mysterious voice revealed itself as a Yaksha and warned Yudhishthira against using the water without answering a series of questions. In his characteristic humility and wisdom, Yudhishthira acknowledged the Yaksha and offered to respond to the questions to the best of his abilities.

The subsequent conversation between Yudhishthira and the Yaksha delved into profound philosophical inquiries, touching upon topics such as the origins of the sun, the nature of intelligence, the pursuit of knowledge, and the reconciliation of conflicting desires. Yudhishthira answered each question humbly, drawing upon his deep understanding of dharma and unwavering commitment to righteousness.

Throughout the conversation, Yudhishthira's character remains consistent and steadfast. His humility, wisdom, and commitment to dharma shine through, guiding his responses and actions. He exemplifies the ideal of a righteous leader who upholds moral principles even in the face of adversity.

Yudhishthira's character, as revealed in his conversation with the Yaksha, serves as a moral compass for individuals seeking to navigate the complexities of life. His unwavering commitment to dharma, humility, and pursuit of knowledge and wisdom provide valuable lessons for all who aspire to lead a righteous and meaningful existence.

Yudhishthira's prowess and character are beautifully revealed through his responses to the profound questions posed by the Yaksha. Let us delve into each question and explore the qualities that showcase his remarkable nature:

1. Who is considered alive even after death?

Yudhishthira's answer reveals his deep understanding of the eternal nature of life. He states that a person who has righteous sons is considered alive even after death. This response highlights Yudhishthira's emphasis on righteousness and the importance he places on carrying forward one's values and principles through future generations.

1. What is heavier than the earth?

Yudhishthira's profound response demonstrates his empathy and compassion. He states that a mother's sorrow is heavier than the earth. This answer reflects his sensitivity towards the emotions and pain experienced by a mother, emphasizing his deep understanding of human suffering and the value he places on familial bonds.

1. What is faster than the wind?

Yudhishthira's answer reveals his insightful understanding of the human mind. He states that the mind is faster than the wind. This response showcases his keen perception and awareness of the inner workings of the human psyche, highlighting his mental agility and analytical prowess.

1. What is more numerous than grass?

Yudhishthira's response reflects his deep philosophical insight. He states that thoughts are more numerous than grass. This answer demonstrates his contemplative nature and his ability to recognize the infinite possibilities and complexities that exist within the realm of human consciousness.

1. What is the greatest wonder?

Yudhishthira's answer to this question showcases his contemplation of life's mysteries and his profound wisdom. He states that the greatest wonder is the fact that countless people die day after day, yet the living continue to believe they will live forever. This response highlights his recognition of the impermanence of life and his understanding of the human tendency to cling to the illusion of immortality.

1. What is the most wonderful thing?

Yudhishthira's response to this question reflects his introspective nature and spiritual depth. He states that the most wonderful thing is the fact that even though countless beings die every moment, people still hold onto the belief that they will live forever. This answer reveals his contemplation of life's transience and the paradoxical nature of human perception.

1. What is the eternal duty of a human being?

Yudhishthira's answer reveals his unwavering commitment to righteous conduct and self-improvement. He states that the eternal duty of a human being is to constantly engage in self-improvement and the pursuit of knowledge. This response underscores his dedication to personal growth and his belief in the transformative power of continuous learning.

1. Who is a true warrior?

Yudhishthira's response to this question emphasizes his understanding of the qualities that define a noble warrior. He states that a true warrior possesses forgiveness, self-control, and righteousness. This answer highlights his belief in the importance of inner strength, moral integrity, and the ability to rise above conflict with grace and compassion.

1. Who is the friend of a person in distress?

Yudhishthira's answer reflects his profound insight into the true source of support and solace in times of distress. He states that a person's own knowledge and righteous conduct are their true friends in moments of hardship. This response showcases his belief in the power of inner resilience and the significance of one's own virtues in navigating challenging circumstances.

One of the most crucial moments in the conversation between Yudhishthira and the Yaksha occurs when Yudhishthira is faced with a moral dilemma. The Yaksha asks him, "Among your brothers, who do you consider to be the most virtuous?" Yudhishthira, without hesitation, responds, "Nakula."

This answer reveals Yudhishthira's deep devotion to his late aunt Madri, Nakula's mother. Madri was the second wife of Yudhishthira's father, King Pandu, and she tragically died after giving birth to Nakula and his twin brother Sahadeva. Despite not being his biological mother, Madri held a special place in Yudhishthira's heart, and he cherished her memory.

When Yudhishthira chooses Nakula as the most virtuous among his brothers, it highlights his profound love and respect for Madri. Yudhishthira's decision to prioritize Nakula's life reveals his commitment to honoring the memory of his aunt and recognizing the virtues she instilled in her son.

This choice also showcases Yudhishthira's unwavering adherence to dharma and his sense of duty towards his family. He values every life and understands the importance of preserving familial bonds.
