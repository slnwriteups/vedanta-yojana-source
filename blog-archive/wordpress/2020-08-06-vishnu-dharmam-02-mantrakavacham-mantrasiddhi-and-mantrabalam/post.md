# Vishnu Dharmam - 02 - Mantrakavacham, Mantrasiddhi and Mantrabalam

![](images/01-aec967-374f7d6f4736416b86d0124693ab6303.jpg)

The first lesson that is taught by Sri Vishnu Dharmam is how one must reduce the sins that are committed during their life. Shounaka Maharishi tells Shathaneeka that one must first commit themselves to know what their purpose and meaning in life is. If one seeks this, they can find knowledge. Through finding knowledge, they learn about the world and everything nature has to teach. To keep the learning enthusiasm, one must follow some basic daily principles. According to one's Varna-Ashrama, some basic principles are to be followed. For example, if one is born into a brahmin family, its the person's fundamental duty to perform *Trikaala-Sandhyavandhanam.* If one is unable to perform *Trikaala-Sandhyavandhanam,* they must seek out a way to compensate for its effect in their life. To offer the same impact as performing *Trikaala-Sandhyavandhanam's* in the lives of those who are not born into a brahmin family, Shounaka Maharishi tells Shathaneeka to seek the mantras that have the same power and capabilities as performing *Trikaala-Sandhyavandhanam*. The mantras can be recited by anyone regardless of which Varna-Ashrama, age, or gender that they belong to.

Shounaka Maharishi explains to Shathaneeka that there are various ways to achieve multiple mantras. Still, the only way that the mantas will take effect (Mantrakavacham) is if they are received through an Acharya. The Acharya first tests the pupil's capability to control their *Shamam and Dhamam*. *Shamam* refers to the ability of the pupil to control his/her Pancha Karma and Janana Indriyas under any external environmental conditions. *Dhamam* refers to the ability of the pupil to control his Pancha Karma and Jnana Indriyas from the wavering nature of the mind. If a pupil can demonstrate that they are capable of controlling *Shamam and Dhamam,* the Acharya bestows upon the pupil the Mantra that they desire to learn. If, by any chance, the Acharya thinks the pupil is incapable of showing this control, the Acharya has the liberty to test the pupil's concentration and capability to perform a specific task. If the Acharya sees that the Lord, who is present as the Antharyami along with Mahalakshmi, is guiding the pupil and providing a *Jnana-Tejas*, the Acharya will bestow the pupil with the Mantra that they desire to learn.

The Mantrabalam or the real power of any mantra is obtained when one meditates upon the received Mantra to a point where he/she can understand the deep inner meaning that the Mantra has to offer. For example, the Gayatri Mantra is said to the voice of the Vedas. If one meditates upon the Gayatri Mantra, they become capable of receiving, learning, and mastering the Vedas. If one seeks moksham, the mantropadesham of Tirumantram, Dwaya Mantram, and Charama Shlokam is performed. There are many other mantras such as Rama Mantra for *Santhaana-Praapthi,* Gopala Mantra for *Dhana-Praapthi,* Varaha Mantra for *Bala-Praapthi,* and Nrisimha Mantram for *Dhairya-Praapthi*.

For those who are incapable of achieving the various mantras and reaping their benefits, they can perform *Theetha-Snaanam*. *Theertha-Snaanam* refers to bathing in a holy river to wash away any sins that are committed on a day-to-day basis. Finally, if one is unable to perform even *Theertha-Snaanam*, they are advised to visit temples and spend some time under the temple's vimanam or inside the temples mandapam. Spending time under a temple's vimanam or inside a temple mandapam is said to bring numerous benefits. For example, if one spends some amount of time in the Gayatri Mandapam of Sri Rangam, they gain the same amount of punyam as reciting the Gayatri Mantram 1008 times, and if one performs a good deed under the Punyakoti Viamanm of Kanchipuram, the effect of the punyam achieved from completing the task is multiplied over one crore times.
