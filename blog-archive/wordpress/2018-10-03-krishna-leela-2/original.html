नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् । 
देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।

Lord Krishna won the hearts of all the people of Vrindavan in a short period of time. He performed many miracles during his short stay in Vrindavan. Kansa continued his attempt to find Krishna in Vrindavan by sending multiple demons to find and kill Krishna. 
The most prominent of the demons being Agasura, Bakasura, and Dhenukasura.
 Once when Krishna and all his friends were out grazing their cattle, they encountered a cave opening that they hadn't ever encountered before. Krishna's friends entered one after the other but didn't come out. Soon seeing the pattern Krishna realized that this wasn't an ordinary cave opening but Agasura. Agasura had the ability to transform himself into a giant snake and as anything entered his mouth would fall asleep inside and he would close his mouth immediately digesting anything that had entered. Krishna entered his mouth and as soon as he entered Agasura closed his mouth. Krishna made sure that he safely moved his friends and all the cattle to safety after which he started to grow in size. He grew to a size wherein Agasura couldn't contain Krishna as he ripped through Agasura's stomach freeing his friends and cattle. 
On another occasion, Krishna and his friends were playing and Krishna was blindfolded when a huge bird swooped from above and took Krishna in his huge jaws and flew away. Krishna is said to have split his beak in two killing him in the process.
One day Krishna and Balarama were sitting at home when they heard that there was a grove of palm trees nearby which was left untouched because of a demon who used to live near the grove. This demon was none other than Dhenukasura who had the ability to transform himself into a rampageous donkey who kicks had the power to kill. Krishna and Balarama told their friends not to worry and took them to the grove. Soon Dhenukasura realized that someone had entered his grove and were eating the fruit. He transformed into his donkey form and went on a rampage. Balarama grabbed him by the tail and hurled against the face of a nearby hill killing him.

Once Krishna saw that the villagers were offering prayers to Indra so that they could get good rains for the sake of reaping good produce. Krishna stopped them and advised them to worship the Govardhan Hill as it gave lush pastures for their cows to graze and it also gave many different varieties of fruits. The villagers saw Krishna's point and ignored their prayers to Indra and started to worship Govardhan. Indra was enraged by this and flooded Vrindavan with incessant rains which spanned for seven days and nights. When he came to see the outcome of his wrath he saw that all the people were unharmed and were standing under the shelter of Govardhan which was being lifted by Krishna on his little finger of his right hand. Indra upon seeing this realized that nobody who was under the shelter of the Lord could be hurt in any way. He instantly stopped the rains and apologized to the Lord saying his power had blinded him but he has now realized his responsibility. 

Once Lord Krishna was playing ball near the river Yamuna when the ball fell inside. The area was said to be the home Kaliya the Naga who was dwelling in the waters of Vrindavan where he would be safe from his sworn enemy Garuda. Kaliya had begun to spew his venom into the waters of the Yamuna which was claiming the life of cattle and humans alike. Using the fallen ball as an excuse Krishna jumped into the waters of the Yamuna and brought Kaliya to the surface. As soon as Kaliya reached the surface, Lord Krishna jumped onto Kaliya's hood and danced on his hood till he started to bleed and slowly started to die. Seeing their husband's fate Kaliya's wives pleaded to Krishna that he spare their husband. Krishna agreed to spare Kaliya's life if he were to abandon the waters of the Yamuna and return to Ramanaka Dwip (Modern day Fiji). Thus he saved the people of Vrindavan from an impending threat.
