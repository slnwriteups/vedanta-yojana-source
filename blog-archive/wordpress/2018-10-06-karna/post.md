# Karna

**नारायणं** **नमस्कृत्य** **नरं** **चैव** **नरोत्तमम्** **।**

**देवीं** **सरस्वतीं** **व्यासं** **ततो** **जयमुदीरयेत्** **।।**

As we have already seen the birth of Karna in Kunti's story we will now see how he became a formidable warrior who could come toe-to-toe with Arjuna.

Karna was found by Adhiratha the charioteer to Dhritarashtra who raised Karna as his own.

Karna grew up watching the Pandavas and Kauravas and hence took an interest in learning the arts of warfare. He approached Drona to teach him the knowledge of weapons and warfare. Drona refused to teach him saying that he would teach Kshatriyas and since he was the son of a charioteer Karna didn't qualify to be his disciple. Determined to learn the forms of warfare, he approached Lord Parashurama to become his disciple. Lord Parashurama placed a condition saying he would impart his knowledge to Brahmins only. Hearing this condition, in desperation, Karna lied to Lord Parashurama saying he was Brahmin and finished his education. He became a very successful archer who could rival the likes of Arjuna. After completing his education Lord Parashurama reveals to Karna the *Bharghavastra* which is comparable to a modern-day programmable missile. Lord Parashurama gave him the knowledge of the Bharghavastra and immediately felt extremely tired. To serve his guru, Karna offered his lap for Lord Parashurama to rest his head. But while Parashurama was asleep, an insect stung Karna's thigh. Despite the pain, Karna did not move, so as not to disturb his guru.

With blood oozing from his wound, Parashurama woke up at once deduced that Karna was not a Brahmin. Parashurama, who had sworn to teach Brahmins only, laid a curse upon Karna that he would forget all the knowledge required to wield any weapon at a time that it would be required the most.

To display the skills of the Kuru princes, guru Dronacharya arranged a friendly tournament.

Karna arrived at this tournament, uninvited, and surpassing Arjuna's feats, challenged him to a duel. Kripacharya refused Karna his duel, asking first for his clan and kingdom; for according to the rules of dueling, only a prince could challenge Arjuna to a duel. Due to his low standing, Karna was not allowed to fight Arjuna. Duryodhana, the eldest of the one hundred sons of the king Dhritarashtra, knew that his cousins Pandavas were better at warfare. Seeing Karna as a chance to get on even terms with them he immediately offered Karna the throne of the kingdom of Anga, making him a king and hence eligible to fight a duel with Arjuna.

When Karna asked him what he could do to repay him, Duryodhana told him that all he wanted was his friendship.
