# Vishnu Puranam - 10 - Sri Krishna in Mathura

After his multiple failures in his attempts to find and kill Krishna, Kansa sends his chief minister Akroora to Vrindavan to invite Krishna and Balarama to Mathura to participate in a Danur-Yagna accompanied with a wrestling competition with an intent to kill them in Mathura.

![](images/01-aec967-bba7439e8134469992823e858524ce79.jpg)

Akroora was sent to Vrindavan, where he talked to Nanda and Yashodha about the Danur-Yagna function that was taking place in Mathura and that the king had asked Akroora to personally escort Krishna and Balarama to Mathura to take part in the competition. When Yashodha heard this, she was strictly against sending Krishna to Mathura, but Krishna was firmly determined to go and told his parents that he would accept the invitation and go to Mathura. Akroora was to stay the night in Vrindavan for the night, and they would leave first thing in the morning. In the morning, they began the journey to Mathura. By the evening, they decided to spend some time by the banks of the Yamuna to relax before entering Mathura by the morning.

![](images/02-aec967-326cb856d7024dcb98bda432003f9100.jpg)

As Akroora took a dip in the Yamuna, he saw the divine form of Lord Vishnu in Vaikuntham along with Goddess Lakshmi, and as he kept looking in awe, he saw Lord Vishnu taking the form of Krishna and Balarama. Akroora then understood why Krishna was undertaking the journey to Mathura. By morning they reached Mathura. Upon reaching Mathura, Krishna and Balarama got off at the city gates claiming they wanted to see the city before they entered the stadium to participate in the competitions and told Akroora to inform Kansa of their arrival. As they strolled through the town, Krishna performed his first miracle when a deformed woman by the name Trivakra fell to his feet. As Krishna picked her up, he first placed the sole of his foot on Trivakra's foot. Upon doing this, her face and skin deformity first vanished, and as he pulled her up, her hunchback straightened and transformed her into a beautiful maiden. Trivakra then acted as their guide to Mathura.

Trivakra took Krishna and Balarama to the stadium where the Danur-Yagna was taking place. Krishna went to the bow single-handedly, lifted the bow, strung it, and broke it, leaving the crowd stunned. Kansa then devised a plan to kill

![](images/03-aec967-8fd0c235e5b34baa9de9bc77b6882527.jpg)

Krishna the following morning during the wrestling competition. He called the mahoot of his most substantial elephant Koliyapeed. He instructed the mahoot to make sure the elephant gets drunk with alcohol and is set free to go on a rampage, thereby trampling Krishna and Balarama on sight. The following morning outside the stadium, the elephant is set free by the mahoot, where it starts to go on a rampage. To stop any damage from taking place, Balarama caught the elephant by the tail and began to rotate it and hurls the elephant high in the air. Amid the wrestling competition, the elephant comes and lands in the stadium with a colossal thud dying on the spot.

![](images/04-aec967-c6af8604e3d043f8a879856caec2aeac.jpg)![](images/05-aec967-3c9a17ab85974d8598192f05ca759707.jpg)

Krishna and Balarama participate in the wrestling contest and beat Kansa's best wrestling brothers Chanura and Mushtika. Soon Kansa himself faces Krishna in hand-to-hand combat but proves to be no match to his nephew. Lord Krishna slays Kansa and frees his parents and his grandfather from prison.
