नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।

 Yesterday we have seen how space and time are mapped according to our ancient texts. Today we will move on and visit the stories of Jaya and Vijaya and the story of Varaha Avataram.

 Shuka Maharishi narrates to Parikshith the story of when Vidura met Uddhava who informs Vidura of Shree Krishna’s passing. Vidura then meets Sage Maitreya, who explains to Vidura the various concepts of cosmology then the sage goes on to explain the process of creation and destruction of the world and how the Lord incarnates himself to reconstruct the world at the time of Pralaya. 
The sage then narrates the incident wherein, the sons of Brahma, The Four Kumaras wished to see the Lord at Vaikuntham. Here when I talk about Vaikuntham, I’m referring to Karya Vaikuntham (कार्यवैकुंटम्). 
(Vaikuntham as we know it is the residence of Lord Sriman Narayana, bounded by 108 gates that open to the Ksheerabdhi upon which lies the Lord on his Serpent recliner Adishesha. This has been described as a place of no return. But in many cases, we hear that many gods such as Brahma, Indra and Bhoomi Devi who come seeking the Lord’s assistance in times of need and return to their places after the task is complete. This may seem like an oxymoron, but in reality, there are two Vaikuntham each that resembles the other as an exact replica. The point of no return is called Sri Vaikuntham and that Vaikuntham wherein the gods and revered sages visit the Lord is known as Karya Vaikuntham.)
As the four Kumaras, reached the gates of Vaikuntham, they saw that their path was blocked by the Lord’s guards Jaya and Vijaya. The four sages asked that they are allowed to see the Lord. Jaya and Vijaya immediately said no stating the reason that the Lord was in the state of Yoga Nidhra and he would not be disturbed by anyone. The four sages were enraged and cursed the two guards saying that they were misusing their benefits and that they would be separated from the Lord. The two guards pleaded with the sages to take back their curse but they left in a fury. The two then approached the Lord and pleaded with him to remove the curse that was placed upon them. The Lord told them that it wouldn’t be possible but there were two options that could be offered to carry out the curse. The first option that he gave them was that they would have to reincarnate seven times as the friends of the lord and then get back to Vaikuntham or the second option that was given was they would reincarnate three times but as demonic forces who would die at his hands and return to Vaikuntham. Jaya and Vijaya thought for a while and told the Lord that seven reincarnations would mean they would be separated from him for far too long. So they chose to reincarnate as demonic forces three times and satisfy the curse that was cast upon them. They would go on to incarnate themselves as i) Hiranyaksha and Hiranyakashipu ii) Ravana and Kumbhakarna and iii) Shishupala and Danthavakra. 

 Once during the Pralaya process of a Krita Yuga, Hiranyaksha kidnapped Bhoomi Devi and took her captive deep with the waters of the deluge. Brahma became worried at this point. If Bhoomi Devi ceases to exist, then there would be nobody to bear the brunt of the Pralaya process and creation would be at a setback. At this time Brahma exhaled with a deep sigh and from one of his nostrils emerged a tiny wild boar that grew into a gigantic size. The boar then bore long tusks, Grew four arms and held a mace in one of the four hands and went in pursuit of Bhoomi Devi. The Lord rescues Bhoomi Devi by carrying her with his tusks and places her back in the position where she was to rest. Soon Hiranyaksha receives news that a boar bearing a mace had rescued Bhoomi Devi. Hiranyaksha in a fury rushes to find and recapture Bhoomi Devi when he comes face to face with Lord. Lord Varaha and Hiranyaksha fight continuously for hundreds of years and finally, the Lord slays Hiranyaksha. After this, the Lord is married to Bhoomi Devi.
