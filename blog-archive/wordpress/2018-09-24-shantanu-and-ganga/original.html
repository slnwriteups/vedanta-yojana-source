नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् । 
देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।
Shantanu was a king of the mighty Chandra-Vamsha who ruled the land of Hastinapur. One day King Shantanu was leisurely taking a stroll on the banks of the river Ganga when he saw a beautiful maiden on the river bank. Drawn by her beauty he asks her to marry him. She accepts his marriage proposal but places a condition saying he must not question any of her actions after they get married. Upon agreeing to the placed condition, they get married. A few months pass and Ganga gives birth to a baby boy whom she immediately drowns in the river. Shantanu although, devastated couldn't question her actions as he was bound to his promise given to Ganga before their wedding. One by one Ganga gave birth to seven children all of which she drowned in the river. As she was about to drown the eighth child Shantanu couldn't control himself and confronted Ganga. He said to her that he remained quiet as she drowned the previous seven children but no more. To this Ganga explains that she was simply fulfilling a part of a curse given to her by Lord Brahma. She narrates the story of Shantanu's past life where he was a king named Mahabhisha. Mahabhisha was a mighty ruler. To show his dominance he had performed a thousand Ashwamedha Yagnas and a hundred Rajasuya Yagnas after which he attained swarga, a paradise which only the gods could experience. Once, he got an opportunity to visit Brahmaloka ( the residence of Lord Brahma) where all the gods and even Ganga had assembled. As the gods were delivering their prayers to Lord Brahma a wind blew by and displaced Ganga's clothing. All the gods bashfully bent their heads except for Mahabhisha who continued gazing at Ganga. Seeing this Lord Brahma lost his temper and cursed Mahabhisha to be reborn as a mortal. Ganga was also cursed as she didn't find any fault in the actions of Mahabhisha. Ganga was cursed to also be born a mortal and to break Mahabhisha's heart so that it would set an example to all people that the actions of Mahabhisha were unethical. Then she told him that their eight children were Eight Vasus who were cursed by Vasishtha to be born on earth as mortal humans however when they pacified him, he limited his curse and told them that they would be freed from this curse within a year of their birth as humans. So she released the seven of them from this life by drowning them all. However, the Vasu Dayus was cursed to live a long life and to never have a wife or have children. But the sage also gave a boon to him that he would be virtuous, conversant with all the holy scriptures and will be an obedient son to his father. that she will take him to the heavens to train him properly for the King's throne and status. With these words, she disappeared along with the child while Shantanu was struck with grief thinking about spending the rest of his life without her.
Shantanu, filled with grief from the loss of his wife and son, began to practice Brahmacharya and ruled his kingdom extremely well. By merely adopting the virtuous behavior, Shantanu was easily able to conquer the entire world without lifting weapons. All the kings declared Shantanu as Emperor and his reign was a peaceful one. Shantanu gave up hunting and gained popularity from his subjects. One day, while walking along the banks of the Ganga, Shantanu saw that the river had become shallow. While searching for the cause of this phenomenon, he came across a handsome young boy who had checked the river's flow with his celestial weapon. The young boy was his son, however, he didn't recognize him because he could see him only for a few moments after he was born. The boy recognized that he was his father, however, he didn't reveal it to him instead he disappeared from his sight using his power of illusion. Shantanu upon seeing this wondered whether the boy was actually his son and called upon Ganga to show the boy to him. Ganga having thus appeared revealed to him that the boy was actually his son Devavrata and that he was taught the knowledge of the holy scriptures by the sage Vasishtha and the art of warfare by Lord Parashurama. After revealing the truth about Devavrata she told Shantanu to take him to Hastinapura. Upon reaching the capital Shantanu crowned Devavrata as the heir-apparent to the throne.
