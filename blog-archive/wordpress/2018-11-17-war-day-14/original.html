नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।


As day fourteen of the war began, Dronacharya once again had prepared the Chakravyuha formation and kept Jayadrata hidden under the protection of Ashvatthama and Kripacharya. 
Arjuna attacked the Chakravyuha with a fury that had never been seen before. Arjuna took out an entire akshauhini on that day. Jayadrata remained in hiding the entire day as Arjuna had vowed to jump into a pyre if he wasn't able to kill Jayadrata. At close to sunset, Krishna released his chakra and set it up in such a way that it would cover the sun thereby simulating sunset. As soon as Arjuna saw that the sun was not visible in the sky he came to a conclusion that the sun had already set and so dismounted his chariot and by the means of one of his many divine weapons, summoned Agni Deva so as to make his journey into the afterlife, one that would be holy. To witness Arjuna enter the pyre all the great warriors from both sides assembled. Arjuna began to take his parikrama around the flame while paying his respects to the divine flame. When Jayadrata heard that Arjuna was going through with his vow, he was elated and filled with a sense of confidence and greed that he would be the one who would turn the course of the war. He arrived at the scene be left of his weapons to ridicule Arjuna as he would make his final journey. As soon as Jayadrata showed himself at the scene, Lord Krishna moved his chakra and immediately stopped Arjuna. He told Arjuna to raise his gaze and told him that the sun was still very much in the sky, with his sworn enemy in front of him. Krishna encouraged Arjuna to once again raise his Gandiva and behead Jayadrata in such a way that his head falls in his father's lap. Jayadrata had a boon given by his father stating that when Jayadrata is killed and his head falls down on the ground the last person who the head sees would explode to ashes. Arjuna summons the Pashupathastra and beheads Jayadrata in such a way that his head falls onto his father's lap who was offering his evening prayers. The head landed on Jayadrata's father's lap and as he rose the head fell to the ground and he fell prey to the boon given to his son.
As Arjuna was getting his way around the Chakravyuha, Bheema was decimating the Kauravas. He killed twenty-one brothers that day including Vikarna who was the only one among the hundred brothers who hadn't supported the disrobing of Draupadi and he was also the only warrior on the Kaurava side who left the battlefield in disgust after the killing of Abhimanyu. Bheema reluctantly fought Vikarna but Vikarna was a man of promises. He wanted to make sure that Bheema keeps up his vow to kill all the hundred brothers and thus constantly instigated Bheema to attack him by reminding him of Draupadi's disrobing. Bheema understood what Vikarna was up to and in a fierce battle of the mace, Bheema stood victorius but later condemned Vikarna's death as a sad tragedy.
War should have ended at sunset but it continued on that day. Drona went on to kill Drupada and Virata. As the sun set, Gatothkacha’s power grew immensely. He started to go on an onslaught and decimated the Kaurava Army. Duryodhana started to get concerned and asked Karna to use the Shakthi-Astra that was presented to him by Indra. At first, Karna was hesitant as he was reserving the weapon to use against Arjuna. But soon he saw the possibility that if he didn't use the Shakthi, the Battle would most likely end that night. Hesitantly Karna summoned the Shakthi and used the weapon against Gatothkacha. As the weapon struck him, Gatothkacha grew to a ginormous size and even in his dying breath took an entire akshauhini along with him.
