नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् । 
देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो जयमुदीरयेत् ।।

Devavrata was the eighth child born to Shantanu and Ganga. Upon his birth, he was taken by his mother to be brought up and trained by many eminent sages.
Brihaspati: The preceptor of the Devas taught Devavrata the duties of kings (Dandaneeti), or political science and other Shastras.
Shukracharya: The preceptor of the Asuras also taught Devavrata in political science and other branches of knowledge.
Vashishtha: The Brahmarshi, taught the Vedangas and other holy scriptures to Devavrata who mastered the Vedas.
Sanatkumara: The eldest son of Lord Brahma taught Devavrata the mental and spiritual sciences.
Parashurama:  Parashurama trained Bhishma in warfare.
Indra: The king of the Devas, bestowed celestial weapons on Bhishma.
Bhishma means “one who took a terrible oath” referring to his oath to be a brahmacharya till the end of his life. He took this oath so that his father could be remarried to Satyavati. Satyavati’s father had refused to give his daughter’s hand in marriage to Shantanu as none of her children would inherit the throne of Hastinapur. This made Shantanu feel very disheartened and upon, discovering the reason Devavrata rushed to Satyavati’s father and made a vow that he would never claim a stake to the throne, therefore, making way for Satyavati and Shantanu’s children to become the ruler after Shantanu. At this, Satyavati's father retorted that even if Devavratha gave up his claim to the throne, his (Devavratha's) children would still claim the throne. Devavrata then took the vow of being a brahmacharya his entire life thus sacrificing his position as heir-apparent to the throne. This gave him recognition among the gods and his father granted him the boon of Iccha- Marani ( control over one’s death – he could choose his time of death).
Soon criticism started to pour in from the subjects of Hastinapur expressing their grief of Devavrata’s removal as heir-apparent as he was a very capable prince. There was worry about Shantanu’s unborn children who were now promised the throne. Hearing the people’s concern, Bhishma said it was his decision and his father wasn’t to be held accountable as he had never promised anything to Satyavati’s father. The amatya ( prime minister) of the royal court then asked who would be held accountable if the future heir apparent isn’t capable enough. Bhishma then took another vow that he would always see his father’s image in whomsoever sat on the throne and would thus serve them faithfully.
