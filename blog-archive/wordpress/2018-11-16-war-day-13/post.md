# War- Day 13

**नारायणं नमस्कृत्य नरं चैव नरोत्तमम् ।

देवीं सरस्वतीं व्यासं ततो** **जयमुदीरयेत् ।।**

After Dronacharya’s failing twice to capture Yudhishthira Duryodhana starts to lose faith and severely reprimands Drona for his incapacity. Drona remained unfazed by the comments being passed against him and says he would go through with the plan to form the Chakravyuha the next day and make sure Arjuna is away from the main battlefield so that they could capture Yudhishthira easily, without any problems. At dawn, the Samsaptaka clan members issue a challenge to Arjuna saying if he wasn't a coward he would end their feud with a fight to the death by the end of that day. Arjuna who accepted the challenge was drawn southwards of the main battlefield where the most fierce battle of the war that had already perspired and what was to happen during the war to come took place. Whereas, on the main battlefield Drona set up the Chakravyuha. The Pandava army was taken aback when they saw the formation of the Chakravyuha in front of them. Arjuna and Shree Krishna were the only two people on the Pandava Army who possessed the knowledge of penetrating the Chakravyuha and getting out safely. Yudhisthira was lost for a moment and then the silence in the ranks of the Pandava Army was broken by the young Abhimanyu. He told his uncles and the other assembled warriors that he possessed the knowledge of penetrating the Chakravyuha but was incapable of getting out of the formation. Yudhisthira and Bheema were elated at the statement of their nephew and told him to make a path to penetrate the formation and they would follow in close proximity and assist him to get out safely. With this statement of confidence, Abhimanyu started his attack.
Abhimanyu got through the outer circumferential of the formation and as he went on to penetrate the inner circles, Jayadrata blocked the path of the Pandavas. Jayadrata was at an advantage that day as he chose to change the course of the war in favor of the Kauravas. With Arjuna away, he used the boon given by Indra to block the Pandavas. He fought the four brothers without any problem and emerged victorious in any form of battle he waged with them may it have been with the bow and arrow, the mace, the sword, the javelin or hand to hand combat. As Jayadrata kept the Pandavas distracted the brave Abhimanyu progressively broke into the inner ranks of the formation. At one point Abhimanyu had reached a stage where he was faced with a great warrior in front of him and to enter the subsequent layer of the formation had to beat that warrior. Abhimanyu outclassed every warrior who was placed in front of him. May the warrior had been Dushasana, Karna, Shakuni, Ashvatthama, Kripacharya, Kritavarma or even Drona himself, they were left defenseless in front of Abhimanyu. Soon Duryodhana's son Lakshmana challenged Abhimanyu. But before challenging him took a vow to his father that he would place Abhimanyu’s head at his father’s feet. Abhimanyu without any mercy fought Lakshmana and with a shaft took off Lakshmana’s head and sent it flying straight to Duryodhana's feet. Upon seeing the head at his feet Duryodhana was filled with joy and in pleasure lifted the head to see it was none other than his own son. Duryodhana’s anger knew no bounds. He commanded Drona, Karna, Kripacharya, Kritavarma, Dushasana, and Ashvatthama to attack him all at once and if necessary strike him down from behind. Drona suggested to first derail Abhimanyu's chariot and kill his charioteer. This making it easier for them to strike him down. Karna killed Abhimanyu's charioteer and cut of the reigns that were holding his horses to the chariot and from behind Karna released a shaft that broke Abhimanyu's bow. Kritavarma and Kripacharya together destroyed the chariot bringing Abhimanyu to the ground. Even though he was at the mercy of the warriors around him, Abhimanyu drew his sword and shield and defended himself valiantly. With another shaft, Kripacharya disarmed the sword and Karna decimated Abhimanyu's shield.
Left with no weapon, Abhimanyu took a wheel of his chariot and started to hurl it like a discus. It was as though the second coming of Lord Vishnu with his discus Sudharshana. Soon the wheel of the chariot was destroyed and Abhimanyu was left vulnerable to any attack. All the warriors thus in a group attack left Abhimanyu in a pathetic condition. Even after all this Abhimanyu continued to get up and face the warriors in front of him. Dushasana's son was sent to finish the task. The two fought for a long while and both of them collapsed for a while. Abhimanyu was struggling to get back on his feet when the final blow was delivered on his head of the mace of Dushasanas' son. All the warriors gathered around the young sixteen-year-old boy's body and went around in rounds of celebration as a hunter would go around a dead prey. As the savages they all were, they blew the conch on Abhimanyu's death and declared the war closed for the day. After Arjuna defeated the Samsaptaka he went back to his camp and saw the depressed faces of his brothers and all of the warriors who had gathered that night. When he questioned everyone as to what was the reason for their long faces, he got no answer. Finally, he asked Yudhishthira what the matter was and found out that his son was murdered in cold blood and that Jayadrata was the one who was responsible for blocking their path. Arjuna was heartbroken and infuriated at the same time. He then took a vow stating he would kill Jayadrata by sunset the next day else he would make a pyre and jump into it.
