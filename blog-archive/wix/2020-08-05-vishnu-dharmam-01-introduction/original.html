Vishnu Dharmam is a Grantham that was given to Shathaneeka, the son of Janmejaya at Naimisharanyam by Shounaka Maharishi. This Grantham came as an answer to the questions posed by Shathaneeka as to how one must lead life ideally and rule the kingdom. The principles that are mentioned in this Grantham, if followed, will give the follower positive karma, thereby making the journey towards moksham somewhat easier. This Grantham consists of numerous (a story within the story ), which teaches us different morals that are to be followed. Before entering the stories that are mentioned in Vishnu Dharmam, we will first see the heritage of Shathaneeka and his lineage. As mentioned earlier, Shathaneeka is the son of Janmejaya, who was the son of Parikshith, who was the son of Abhimanyu, who was the son of Arjuna. Parikshith allowed the world to receive Srimad Bhagavata Mahapuranam. The story of the Bhagavata-Mahapuranam begins with the end of the Mahabharata war. After the eighteenth day of the war, Ashwatthama had pledged to Duryodhana to eliminate the Pandavas and their lineage. To fulfill his promise, Ashwatthama killed the sons of the Pandavas, who were born of Draupadi and had set his sights on the Pandavas next. As he tried to kill the Pandavas, he realized that he was easily being overpowered and did not stand a chance if he did not take any drastic measures to protect himself. Ashwatthama summoned the Brahmashiras-Astra and threatened to destroy all of creation. It was then that Lord Krishna intervened and told Arjuna also to summon his Brahmashiras-Astra to neutralize that released by Ashwatthama. Before the two weapons could collide, Maharishi Vyasa came in between and told Arjuna and Ashwatthama that the magnitude of the impact of the collision of the two weapons would destroy the heavens above, the earth in the middle and hell below. To avoid this, Vyasa advised the warriors to withdraw their weapons. Arjuna immediately withdrew his weapon, but Ashwatthama reveals that he did not possess the knowledge to withdraw his weapon, but he could change the direction of the weapon. Maharishi Vyasa advises Ashwatthama to direct his weapon in a direction wherein there were no living beings, where his weapon cannot cause any destruction. In a last attempt to fulfill his promise, Ashwatthama directs his weapon towards the womb of Uttara, the wife of Abhimanyu, who was carrying a child who would go on to become the sole heir to the Pandava lineage. Arjuna then pleads with Lord Krishna to save the baby. To protect the baby, it is said that the Lord took on a minuscule size and entered Uttara's womb. The Lord started to go around in circles around the baby and absorbed the impact of the weapon saving the baby. As the Lord was going around the baby, it is said that the baby was consciously looking to find out why he wasn't alone in the womb and thus followed the circular motion of the Lord hence getting the name Parikshith. (परिः ईक्षति इति परिक्षित) Parikshith, who was named the successor to the throne of the Kuru lineage, became a mighty and respected king. One day Kali Yuga took a human form and confronted Parikshith, who was the last of the rulers from the Dwapara Yuga whom Kali had still not infected in thought and action. Parikshith was a dominant personality, and Kali failed to poison his mind. Kali then pretended to leave Parikshith and his kingdom but, in reality, placed its essence within Parikshith's s crown. Hence, as slowly poison his minds with the contradictory ideas of changing times. A few days later, Parikshith was out on a hunting expedition and, at one point, felt extremely thirsty. He looked around and noticed a Rishi who was meditating under a tree. Parikshith at first approached the sage with utmost etiquette and respect, but when the sage showed no response, by the influence of Kali, in his head, saw the freshly shed skin of a snake, which he flung upon the sage. The sage turned out to be Shamika Maharishi. The incident of the flinging of the snakeskin was noticed by the friend of the son of the Maharishi, who tells the son. The son in anger lays a curse upon Parikshith, saying he would die in seven days by the bite of a snake as he had used the snake's skin to insult his father. Parikshith wanted a way to spend these last seven days of his life in a meaningful manner. Parikshith handed over the throne to his son Janamejaya and went to the Ashrama of the great Shuka Maharishi, who was the son of Maharishi Vyasa. Here at the Ashrama, Parikshith narrates the story of what had perspired and asked the great sage to help him make the remaining of his life meaningful. When Parikshith asked this of the great sage, arrangements were made wherein many sages assembled at the Ashrama, and Shuka Maharishi narrates Srimad Bhagavatam to Parikshith over seven days. Janmejaya was determined to avenge his father's death and decided that he would vanquish all snakes from existence. To do this, Janmejaya started to performed a yagam known as Sarpasatram in which all the snakes in existence would be drawn into the sacrificial flame and die. As Janmejaya was performing this yagam, Vyasa and Vaishmapayana Maharishis came and stopped him saying his father's karma had come to an end and that he was destined to die in the way that he had. They also told Janmejaya that it was inappropriate to vanquish all the snakes for what was already predetermined by fate. As Janmejaya had to stop the yagam before it attained its goal, Janmejaya started to experience a certain kind of eczema in which he began to shed his skin like that of a snake. It is said that he visited Guruvayoor and was cured of this condition through the Lord's grace. After Janmejaya's death, Shathaneeka was named the successor throne. Before he began his rule, he made a journey to Naimisharanyam to visit Shounaka Maharishi, who was the pupil of Vaishampayana Maharishi, to gain clarity on how one must live life and rule, thus giving rise to the Grantham Vishnu Dharmam.
