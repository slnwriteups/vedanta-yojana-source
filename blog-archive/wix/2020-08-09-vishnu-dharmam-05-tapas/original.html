In our previous entry, we saw the concept of Dhaanam. Now we shall see the idea of Tapas. According to Shounaka Maharishi, anyone can become a tapsvi during their lifetime. When we hear the word tapasvi, we should not imagine the great rishis or munis. Being a tapasvi is a process and a way of life. As said earlier, anyone can perform Tapasand become a tapasvi if they follow some simple discipline. The following are to be followed to become a tapasvi:

  1. Tyaagam: Performing sacrifice or being bereft of worldly pleasures, which would otherwise be a way of day to day life.

  2. Jnanam: Knowing as to when and what kind of tyaagam has to be done. For example, the shastras prescribe that one must fast during Ekadashi. Here in this situation, one must have the knowledge to fast on that day. The tyaagam performed in this situation is not eating food.

  3. Shamam-Dhamam: As discussed in our previous entry, one must show the capability to have control over both his/her body and mind through the pancha karma and jnana indriyas.

  4. Aalochanam: This refers to the ability of the person to think deeply about the factors as mentioned above and implement them daily.

If one practices those as mentioned above, they are said to be bestowed with the following phalam:

  1. Aayu: Long life

  2. Bhogam: Enjoying the karma-phalam

  3. Jnanam: Ability to grasp knowledge increases

  4. Vijnanam: From jnanam arises the curiosity to learn, which is known as vijnanam.

  5. Aasthikyam: The want to learn more about the Lord and the wish to connect to spirituality.

  6. Roopam-Uthamam: A bright glow (Brahma-tejas) is seen throughout the person's being.

  7. Sarvam-Vindhathe: With the chain of events being set in motion, one will ultimately achieve all of their goals and aspirations, finally leading them to a happy, satisfied life setting precedent to achieve moksham.
