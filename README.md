### A Journey That Began Long Before the App

On 24 September 2018, I published my first piece of writing on a small Blogspot blog.

At the time, I had no idea where it would lead.

I had begun writing about the Mahābhārata—a subject that had fascinated me for years. Those early writings would eventually grow into my first book, Jaya: A Journey of the Mahābhārata.

But the story of Vedanta Yojana really begins much earlier than 2018.

I grew up listening to stories.

Some of my earliest memories are of sitting with my late maternal grandparents as a young boy, perhaps three or four years old, listening to stories from the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, Bhāgavata Purāṇa, and countless other narratives that formed part of the world I was growing up in.

I was fascinated by them.

The stories were not simply stories to me. They contained people, choices, dilemmas, devotion, philosophy, morality, triumphs, failures, questions and consequences. The more I heard, the more I wanted to understand.

And as I grew older, I began to notice something.

Many people around my age simply did not know these stories.

Sometimes my interest in them made me the person in a friend group who was considered unusually “religious” or spiritually inclined. At times, it even became something to be teased about.

But despite that, I always had a feeling that these stories mattered.

They were part of the cultural inheritance I had received from the generations before me.

And somewhere in the back of my mind, I kept thinking:

How do we preserve this?

I didn’t know the answer.

I didn’t even know what I was eventually going to build.

I only knew that I wanted to do something.

⸻

### From a Blog to Books

The blog was my first step.

I started writing, learning as I went.

The platforms changed over time.

Blogspot became Blogger.
Blogger became WordPress.
WordPress eventually became Wix.

The platforms changed, but the writing continued.

Along the way, I also started the Vedanta Yojana Instagram page, experimenting with shorter forms of writing, reflections and microblogging.

Then I discovered Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP).

For the first time, publishing a book was no longer something that seemed distant or inaccessible.

I could write.

I could prepare the manuscript.

I could publish it.

And so I did.

One book became two.

Two became three.

Eventually, the number became five.

Each book represented another piece of a journey that had started with a young boy listening to stories from his grandparents and wondering why these narratives had captured his imagination so completely.

Yet, even after publishing those books, something continued to bother me.

I wasn’t completely satisfied.

⸻

### Wanting to Do More

Publishing through Amazon gave me an avenue to make my books available, but I increasingly wanted something different.

I wanted to have a more hands-on relationship with my work.

I wanted to determine how the content was organized.

I wanted to bring different pieces of my writing together.

I wanted the stories, books, ideas and resources to exist somewhere that was not simply a collection of individual books on a marketplace.

Most importantly, I wanted to create something that could grow.

Something that could become a home for the work.

That idea gradually evolved into something much larger than publishing books.

It became the idea of building a digital platform for knowledge, literature, travel and exploration rooted in Sanātana Dharma.

And that is where Vedanta Yojana began to take shape.

⸻

### The Influence of My Acharyas and Teachers

This journey was also shaped by the people from whom I have had the privilege of learning.

I owe a deep debt of gratitude to Sri U.Ve. Dushyanth Sridhar.

His upanyāsams, teachings and courses through Abhinava e-Patashala became an important building block in my own journey.

Through digital platforms, I was able to experience traditional teachings in a form that could reach someone wherever they happened to be. That experience helped me recognize something important:

Digital media could be a vehicle for preserving and sharing knowledge rather than merely a source of entertainment.

The ability to encounter traditional knowledge through contemporary technology helped me begin imagining how my own writing and content could eventually exist in different digital forms.

That influence became part of the foundation for what Vedanta Yojana would eventually become.

The journey from listening to stories as a child, to writing about them, to publishing books, and finally to attempting to build a digital platform around them did not happen in isolation.

It was shaped by the teachers from whom I learned, the people who encouraged me, and the traditions that gave me something worth preserving in the first place.

⸻

### From Writing to a Digital Yojana

The name Vedanta Yojana existed before the app.

But the app represents the next stage of what that name has come to mean to me.

It is not simply an e-book reader.

It is not simply a travel guide.

It is not simply a library.

It is an attempt to bring several parts of the journey together in one place.

At its heart is a digital library—a place where my books and future works can live, evolve and become accessible beyond the limitations of traditional publishing.

Alongside that is a Sanātana Dharma travel guide, beginning with the 108 Divya Desams, providing information intended to help people discover these sacred places, understand their traditions and eventually plan journeys around them.

The larger vision is to bring together:

* Books and literature
* Stories and knowledge
* Sanātana Dharma
* Temple and pilgrimage information
* The 108 Divya Desams
* Travel and yātrā
* Calendars and important observances
* Writing and reflections
* Future educational and cultural resources

The goal is not to separate these things into isolated pieces.

The goal is to allow them to exist together.

Because reading about a place can lead to wanting to visit it.

Learning a story can lead to wanting to understand its tradition.

Understanding a tradition can lead to wanting to travel.

And a journey can lead back to knowledge.

That cycle is central to the idea behind Vedanta Yojana.

## What Is Vedanta Yojana?

Vedanta Yojana is a digital platform for Sanātana Dharma literature,
sacred-place records, and travel/yātrā planning. It began as a personal
writing project and has grown into a shared content model — the same
validated content — served through both a website and a native Android
app.

At its core today are two things: a digital library of original books,
and a travel guide to the 108 Divya Desams. Both are meant to grow into
a larger whole, as described in [The Journey](#the-journey) above.

## Current Features

Everything below reflects what is actually implemented, not planned:

- **108 Divya Desams** — complete temple records, each available in
  English, Tamil, Kannada, Hindi, and Telugu, with regional classification
- **Digital library** — 4 full-length books (227 chapters): *A Brief
  Insight to Visishtadvaita Philosophy*, *Sri Rama Charithram*, *Srimad
  Bhagavata Kathasagaram*, and *JAYA: A Journey of the Mahabharata*, plus
  a Knowledge section — readable online and downloadable for offline
  reading
- **Offline Pasurams** — Pasuram PDFs for all 108 Divya Desams in
  Sanskrit, English, Tamil, Kannada, and Telugu (540 in total, sourced
  from Prapatti.com), bundled directly into the app; no network
  connection required
- **Search** — across Divya Desams, the Library, and Knowledge records
- **Daily Panchangam** — daily Panchangam and Sankalpam for the place
  you are in, named on screen ("Chennai"), not an assumed fixed city
- **Divya Desam Spotlight** — a daily-rotating featured temple on the
  home screen
- **Reading preferences** — font scale, light/dark theme, reading-position
  memory, and bookmarks
- **Multilingual interface** — English, Tamil, Kannada, Hindi, and
  Telugu, consistent across both web and mobile
- **Over-the-air updates (Android)** — from v16 (1.0.3) on, content and
  JavaScript fixes reach installed apps through EAS Update, with no
  reinstall; verified end to end on a physical device

## Technology

- **Web** — Next.js (static export via `output: "export"`), deployed to
  a custom domain over GitHub Pages
- **Mobile** — Expo (React Native), built and signed via EAS (Expo
  Application Services)
- **Shared content model** — a single, schema-validated content source
  (`content-lib/`) consumed by both front ends, so web and mobile stay
  in sync rather than maintaining duplicate content
- **Language** — TypeScript throughout

Full technical detail: [Development & Architecture](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md).

## Releases

Android releases are published to a dedicated public releases
repository: **[vedanta-yojana-releases](https://github.com/slnwriteups/vedanta-yojana-releases/releases)**.

Each release includes the APK and its SHA-256 checksum. Before
installing, see the [APK Verification Guide](docs/APK-VERIFICATION.md)
to confirm a downloaded file is genuine.

**Upgrading an older install to v16 (1.0.3):**

| Installed version | What to do |
|---|---|
| v13 (1.0.0) | The app shows an "Update available" notice that opens the v16 download; v16 installs over it and keeps your data. |
| v14 (1.0.1) or v15 (1.0.2) | These were debug-signed test builds. The app shows an update notice, but Android will refuse to install v16 over them. Uninstall first (this removes local app data such as bookmarks and downloaded books), then install v16. |
| v10 (1.0.0) or earlier | No update notice appears (v10 checks an address that no longer exists). Download v16 from the releases page; it installs over v10 and keeps your data. |
| v16 | Nothing to do — content and app fixes arrive automatically as over-the-air updates. |

## Security & verification summary

- The Android release is signed using this project's production
  signing credentials, managed remotely by EAS (Expo Application
  Services) — not a local development key.
- Each release on GitHub is published with its SHA-256 checksum and
  signing-certificate fingerprint, so any downloaded APK can be
  independently confirmed — see the
  [APK Verification Guide](docs/APK-VERIFICATION.md). The app is
  distributed only through GitHub, not through any app store.
- Dependency vulnerabilities are tracked via Dependabot and `npm audit`;
  known issues are patched or remediated and covered by automated
  regression tests.
- Full detail: [Security Architecture](docs/SECURITY.md) ·
  [How to verify an APK](docs/APK-VERIFICATION.md) ·
  [Vulnerability reporting](SECURITY.md)

## Privacy & data summary

- No analytics, advertising, crash-reporting, or tracking SDK is
  present in the mobile app's dependencies. This is a statement about
  the **app**, and it remains unconditionally true.
- The **website** (vedantayojana.org) carries cookieless, aggregate
  page-view measurement — no cookies, no client-side storage, no
  cross-site identifier, no individual profiles. Separately, the
  version-check and Library-catalog requests the app already makes to
  that site are counted by country at the network edge; that counting
  observes traffic that already exists and adds nothing whatsoever to
  the app. Full detail, including exactly what is and is not recorded:
  [docs/ANALYTICS.md](docs/ANALYTICS.md).
- No account or sign-in is required or offered.
- Network requests fetch public content (Library book updates, version
  checks) from this project's own GitHub Pages site over HTTPS; nothing
  in the audited source uploads user data anywhere. The source is
  public, so this can be checked directly.
- Location access (coarse/fine) is used only to show the day's
  Panchangam for the user's approximate location, and to name that place
  on screen so the reader can see which place the Panchangam and
  Sankalpam are valid for. On Android the name comes from the device's
  own OS geocoder; on the website, from BigDataCloud's public,
  key-less reverse-geocoding endpoint, which receives the coordinates
  being named and nothing else. See
  [docs/privacy-policy.html](docs/privacy-policy.html).
- Full detail, including a permission-by-permission table: [docs/SECURITY.md](docs/SECURITY.md#android-security).

## Offline functionality

All Pasurams are bundled inside the app itself and work with no network
connection at all — verified on a physical device with Wi-Fi and mobile
data both disabled. Library books you've downloaded remain available
offline; checking for new or updated Library content and checking for
app updates both require connectivity. See
[Pasuram offline architecture](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md#pasuram-offline-architecture).

## The two runtimes

| | Website (repo root) | Mobile (`mobile/`) |
|---|---|---|
| Framework | Next.js (`output: "export"`, fully static) | Expo (React Native) |
| Status | **co-equal target — full feature parity** | **co-equal target — full feature parity** |
| Deploy | GitHub Pages, auto-deploys from `main` | [GitHub Releases](#latest-release) (APK) plus over-the-air updates; GitHub only, no app store |
| Detail | — | see `mobile/README.md` |

Both runtimes now carry the same feature set: content translation
(Tamil/Kannada/Hindi/Telugu), a manual light/dark theme override, reading
preferences (font scale), reading-position memory, bookmarks, a
settings/onboarding flow, book cover art, and a daily-rotating "Divya
Desam Spotlight" card — ported from mobile's implementation onto the
website rather than redesigned, sharing the framework-agnostic pieces
(`content-lib/i18n.ts`, `content-lib/ordering.ts`,
`content-lib/divya-desam-spotlight.ts`, `lib/preferences.ts`,
`lib/ui-strings.ts`, and every `*-context.ts` file) directly. Only the
React Native-specific plumbing (AsyncStorage, `useColorScheme`,
`expo-router`, haptics) has a web-specific equivalent (`lib/storage.ts`,
`components/providers/*`). See `mobile/README.md` for mobile's own
detail; the split of what's shared vs. platform-specific between the
two is documented in each new web file's doc comment, tracing back to
its mobile source.

## Content pipeline

```
content/  →  content-lib/ (schemas + loader + search + i18n)  →  app/ (web) or mobile/app/
```

`content/` is the single source of truth (JSON, validated by
`content-lib/schemas/`). It currently holds:

- **108** Divya Desams
- **4** Books, **227** chapters total: *A Brief Insight to
  Visishtadvaita Philosophy* (51 chapters), *Sri Rama Charithram* (75),
  *Srimad Bhagavata Kathasagaram* (31), *JAYA: A Journey of the
  Mahabharata* (70)
- **1** Knowledge record

Neither runtime reads `content/` directly — both go through
`content-lib/`, and never hardcode content. See `content-lib/README.md`
for the schema/loader contract.

## Getting started

**Website:**
```
npm install
npm run dev            # http://localhost:3000
npm run test:content    # content-lib + schema tests
npm run test:app        # app-layer tests
npm run typecheck
```

**Mobile (the real target — see `mobile/README.md` for full detail):**
```
cd mobile
npm install
npx expo start          # scan the QR code with Expo Go on your phone
node --test tests/*.test.ts
```

**After changing anything under `content/`:** the website picks it up
automatically (reads the directory live), but the mobile app does not —
its content is a build-time snapshot. Always run:
```
node mobile/scripts/generate-content-manifest.ts
```
after adding, editing, or removing content, or the change won't appear
in the mobile app.

## Sharing this repo with a collaborator

The repo is a single unit — there's no way to grant access to just
`mobile/` without splitting it into its own repo. Add collaborators via
GitHub Settings → Collaborators and teams → Add people, with **Write**
access for someone contributing code. For a quick UI/UX review session
with no code access needed, `npx expo start --tunnel` from `mobile/`
shares a live QR code they can open in Expo Go on their own phone.
