# Day 29: Shiṟṟam Shiṟugālē

@verse 29

The gopīs say, "shiṟṟam shiṟugālē vandunnai ccēvittu" – in the very small hours of dawn we come only to serve You, not to claim rights. Having Your darshan, they delight in praising His lotus feet and ask Him to listen to this inner "poruḷ," the true content of their coming. They recall the special favour that Krishna chose to be born in their cowherd clan, "peṟṟam mēyttuṇṇum kulattil piṟandu nī," making their otherwise simple birth supremely meaningful. Because of this shared kulam and affectionate closeness, "kuṟṟēval eṅgaḷai kkoḷḷāmal pōgādu" – He cannot ignore or reject their small, flawed service. They admit that even the request for paṟai on "that day" is only a surface-level ask; underneath, the bond is far deeper.

They confess: "iṟṟai ppaṟai koḷvān anṟu kāṇ kōvindā" – Govinda, understand that we did not come only for this paṟai. Their real aspiration is: "eṟṟaikkum ēḻēḻ piṟavikkum un tannōḍuṟṟōmē āvōm" – for all time, across seven times seven births, we want to remain related to You alone, and "unakkē nām āṭ ceyvōm" – we will perform service for You alone. The closing line is the theological core: "maṟṟai nam kāmaṅgaḷ māṟṟu" – remove all our other desires. Āṇḍāl’s message is that the highest devotees are those who drop material cravings and even spiritual bargaining, and simply serve out of love, without asking for reward. For such people, the Lord Himself takes responsibility to grant moksha and remove every barrier between Himself and them; paṟai, bliss, and liberation all follow naturally from that exclusive, desire-free sambandham.
