# Day 7: Kīshu Kīshenṟeṅgum

@verse 7

This pāsuram shows Āṇḍāl waking another friend by pointing to all the sounds of a village that is already up and busy with Krishna on its mind.

First she draws attention to the birds. All around, the ānaiccāttan birds are crying “kīshu kīshu” as they get ready to fly out in search of food, leaving their little ones in the nest. Their insistent calls cut through the quiet and make it clear that dawn has already broken, yet this girl still lies there as if it were night. Āṇḍāl teasingly calls her “pēy peṇṇē” and asks how she can continue to sleep through such a racket. Then she turns to the women of the village. The married Gopikās are already at work, ornaments jingling as they move. The coins and pendants of their tāli at the neck, and the bangles on their wrists, all clash together in a clear, bright rhythm as they churn curds into butter. With every movement, the fragrance from their flower-decked braids and long hair begins to spread, quietly announcing the freshness of the new morning. All of this is happening right outside, and yet the friend inside refuses to stir.

Finally Āṇḍāl reminds her what she is really ignoring. She calls her nāyaga ppeṇ, the natural leader among the girls, and asks how she can lie there without responding when Krishna’s names are being sung. They are calling out “Nārāyaṇa,” singing of Keshava, the Lord who felled the horse-demon Keshi, and inviting this friend to join that kīrtana. The outer chorus of birds, ornaments, and churning is meant to push her toward the inner chorus of Bhagavān-nāma. On a subtler level, Āṇḍāl hints that the true “wealth” of this awakened village is not just butter and milk, but the Veda-smriti treasure guarded by Āchāryas, churned and explained for seekers, until one clear conviction rises out of it like butter from curds: Sriman Nārāyaṇa is the supreme refuge of all beings, and waking up to that truth is the real purpose of this dawn.
