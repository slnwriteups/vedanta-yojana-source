# Day 22: Aṅgaṇ Mā Ñālattu

@verse 22

Āṇḍāḷ recalls how proud kings of this wide, beautiful earth first came swollen with abhimāna and then, once conquered, gathered humbly beneath Krishna’s bed, waiting to serve. In the same way, she says, “we too have come and placed our heads near the foot of your cot,” having consciously laid aside our own ego and claims of superiority. All the subtle inner narratives of “my status, my beauty, my control” are dropped. The gopīs stand not as equals bargaining with God, but as those who recognize that true safety begins only after the collapse of pride. This is the heart of the pāsuram’s teaching: spiritual awakening starts when the sense of independent authority yields to the Lord’s sovereignty.

From this surrendered posture, the gopīs now beg for eyes that look back. They compare Krishna’s eyelids to little bell-shaped lotus petals, slowly parting like the opening mouths of tiny kiṅgiṇi bells, and request that His lotus-eyes not flash open in full brilliance at once, but unfold gently so they can bear the radiance. When those reddish, cool, wide eyes finally rest on them, it is as though sun and moon rise together in a single glance: sun-like intensity to burn away sins and moon-like coolness to soothe their hearts. The inner message is that once ego is discarded and the soul lies at the Lord’s feet, what is needed is just one steady, compassionate look. That look, like a joint sunrise and moonrise, dissolves the burden of defects and guilt and replaces it with a cool, luminous assurance — the lived sense that the Lord has truly accepted and “released the curse” resting on the soul.
