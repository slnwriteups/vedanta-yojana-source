# Day 8: Kīḻvānam Veḷḷenṟu

@verse 8

This pāsuram shows yet another kind of friend whom Āṇḍāl has to wake: not lazy or hostile, but delightfully argumentative and playful, always ready with a clever reply.

Āṇḍāl and her group have already gathered many girls and now stand outside this girl’s house, calling her to join the pāvai nōmbu. They point to the sky: the eastern horizon has begun to glow, the low-roofed little buffalo sheds are opening, and the buffaloes are setting out in small groups to graze. These are all sure signs that it is early morning. But the friend inside refuses to accept that. She jokes that it is not dawn at all; the brightness in the east comes from the moon-like faces of the girls who have come to wake her, standing shoulder to shoulder and reflecting their pale glow onto the sky. The dark "herds of buffaloes" they claim to see are, she insists, only the white clouds stained by the reflection of the girls’ long black hair, drifting across the western horizon like moving animals.

Āṇḍāl lets her talk but then gently presses the real point. She explains that she has brought with her a very special kind of companion: girls whose joy is not only in reaching the final goal, but in walking the path itself, in the journey of the vratam and the taste of each step of upāya, more than even the ultimate upeya. For such friends, staying outside this girl’s door, arguing and coaxing, is itself a cherished part of their spiritual experience. They have even paused their own forward movement to stand here and keep her from being "left behind." Āṇḍāl reminds her that they are all going to praise the tēvādi dēvan, the Lord of all lords, Krishna who once tore open the mouth of the demon calf and crushed the wrestlers in Mathurā. If they can go together and sing His names, offer their paṟai and their voices, the response from Him will be a simple, overflowing "āvā"—"let it be so, let all you seek be granted." Āṇḍāl’s plea is clear beneath all the teasing: "Come out, luminous one; join us, and taste for yourself the sweetness of reciting His stories and names with this band of friends."
