# Day 10: Nōṟṟu Ccuvarkkam

@verse 10

This pāsuram teases yet another friend who is "too blessed for her own good," using humour to expose spiritual complacency while still holding out the sweetness of Krishna’s company.

Āṇḍāl calls her nōṟṟu ccuvarkkam puguginṟa ammanāy—a girl already tasting heaven, as if she is quietly enjoying the Lord’s presence all by herself inside her room. She and the other girls stand outside, but this friend refuses even to open the door or answer them. So they remind her of what truly draws the Lord close: singing the praises of Nārāyaṇa, the One crowned with sweet-scented tulasi garlands. It is He alone, the puṇṇiyan, who grants the real paṟai—not just the drum of the vow, but the blessing of moksha, the gift of His everlasting companionship.

Then comes Āṇḍāl’s playful exaggeration. She asks if this girl has somehow inherited the famous sleep of Kumbhakarṇa himself, the Rāmāyaṇa giant who once fell into a curse of endless slumber. Has all of that overpowering tuyil come and settled only on her, so that even the call of friends and the thought of Krishna cannot rouse her? Yet even while she jokes, Āṇḍāl’s tone is affectionate: "O you who are so richly endowed, āṟṟa anandal uḍaiyāy, treasure-house of joy, stop swaying about in this heavy sleep, come steadily to the door, and open it for us."

At a deeper level, the verse warns against a certain quiet pride that can creep into bhakti. A devotee may begin to think, "The Lord is already in my heart, I am living in ‘heaven’ now; why trouble myself with effort, with community, with discipline?" Āṇḍāl gently exposes this self-satisfaction. The "locked door" is laziness and ahaṅkāra, and the way to open it is to step out, join the saṅgha of devotees, and pour oneself into praising Krishna whose tulasi crown marks Him as the true giver of paṟai, the Lord who alone can lift the soul into that final, fear-free nearness to Vishṇu.
