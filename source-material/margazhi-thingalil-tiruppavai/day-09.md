# Day 9: Tūmaṇi Māḍattu

@verse 9

This pāsuram shows Āṇḍāl waking yet another friend, this time a deeply beloved girl who is spiritually gifted but “locked in” by her own inertia.

Āṇḍāl first paints the scene. The girl lies on a soft bed inside an upper storey built like a jewel-pavilion, with gem-studded walls, rows of lamps burning all around, and the air thick with incense and fragrance. From outside the closed maṇi-kadavam (jeweled door), Āṇḍāl calls, “O daughter of our uncle, open the latch!” When there is no response, she turns to the girl’s mother, “Māmīr, will you not wake her? Is your daughter mute, or deaf, or has she fallen into some strange enchanted sleep?” The teasing question about a mantra-spell hints that such deep, oblivious slumber is itself like being bound by subtle forces.

At the inner level, the “gem-tower” is the luminous knowledge of the Veda, or even the abode of the Āchārya, shining with the radiance of realised truth. The shut door and lock are the seeker’s own ignorance, carelessness, and lack of firm resolve, which keep that light from reaching the heart. “Māmān magaḷ” becomes the cherished disciple or bhakta who has every privilege but still does not step out. Āṇḍāl’s remedy is simple and profound: stand outside and keep chanting the Lord’s names—Māmāyan, Mādhavan, Vaikunthan—until even this heavy sleep breaks. In other words, let constant nāma-saṅkīrtana and remembrance of Krishna, the great wonder-worker who bears Lakshmī and reigns in Vaikuṇṭha, loosen the inner bolt so that the soul can “open the door,” receive true knowledge, and come out into living, conscious companionship with Vishṇu.
