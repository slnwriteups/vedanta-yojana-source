# Day 23: Māri Malai Muḻaiñjil

@verse 23

Āṇḍāḷ begins with a vivid simile: a mighty lion that has been sleeping inside a mountain cave, now stirred to wakefulness. It shakes off sleep, its mane bristles with fragrance, its hair stands on end, it looks fiercely in all directions and steps out with majestic strides, roaring as it comes. This is not a random forest image but a deliberate echo of “yaśōdai iḷam śiṅgam” from the first pāsuram, now expanded. Krishna is being compared to the Lord as Narasiṁha – not to a mere animal lion – the One who sprang from the pillar for Prahlāda’s sake and destroyed Hiraṇyakaśipu. Āṇḍāḷ prays that just like that awakened lion, Krishna with the bluish flower-like hue (pūvaippū vaṇṇan) should emerge from His inner chamber, move out from the “temple” of His private quarters, and seat Himself on His majestic throne (śīriya śiṅgāsanam), glancing at the gopīs with royal grace as He passes.

On the inner plane, this “waking lion” also points to awakened jñāna in the hearts of Ācāryas and true seekers. Just as Narasiṁha arises to tear apart the darkness of adharma, the Lord’s presence in a realized soul rises up to tear apart ignorance in others. Āṇḍāḷ hints that such awakened beings must not remain hidden in their “caves,” but, like the lion, step out, look around, and roar the truth so that others can be guided toward His grace. In this context Nappinnai, who in earlier pāsurams acted as puruṣakāram for the gopīs, is one such path-shower, aiding them to reach Krishna. Āṇḍāḷ herself, through Tiruppāvai, becomes the greatest path-shower for all of us, gifting a work that does exactly what this pāsuram describes: bringing Krishna from “inside” to the siṅgāsanam of our hearts, then inviting Him to listen to our purpose and bless our resolve.
