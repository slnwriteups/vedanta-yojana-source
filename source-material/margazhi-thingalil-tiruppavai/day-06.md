# Day 6: Puḷḷum Śilambina

@verse 6

This pāsuram wakes a half-asleep friend by weaving together village sounds, temple sounds, and the inner call of Krishna’s own līlās. First, Āṇḍāḷ points to the world outside their window. Birds are already awake and chirping, announcing that night has passed and morning has begun. The white conch in the nearby temple of the Lord who rides on Garuḍa is already sounding, its deep, rolling note cutting through whatever drowsiness is left. “Can you not hear that?” she asks her friend. “Wake up, little one!”

She then tells her how they themselves were stirred awake. Sages and yogis, who usually sit absorbed in meditation on the Lord who reclines on Ādiśeṣa in Pāṟkaḍal, have slowly risen from their inward absorption and begun speaking together about Krishna’s childhood wonders: how He drank not only milk but the very life of Pūtanā from her poisoned breast, and how His tiny foot shattered the cart-form of Śakaṭāsura. As they recall and discuss these episodes, they begin to cry out the Lord’s names loudly and lovingly. Those cries, filled with Hari’s name, have entered the girls’ own hearts, cooling and awakening them from within, and now they plead with their friend to let that sound enter her heart too and rise with them.

On a subtler level, Āṇḍāḷ is moving through the five classic forms of the Supreme: the Lord beyond in Vaikuṇṭha (Parama-ātman), the Lord reclining on Śeṣa in the milk ocean (Vyūha), the Lord who once walked as Krishna in Vraja (Vibhava), the Lord present as the consecrated deity in the local temple (Archa), and finally the Lord dwelling as the indwelling Self in the hearts of sages, yogis, and these village girls (Antaryāmin). The sound of the conch hints at the all-pervading “Oṃ,” driving away darkness and calling the soul toward joy, while Pūtanā’s breast points to poisonous attachments that must be uprooted, and Śakaṭa’s cart to the heavy, stubborn mind that needs to be overturned and disciplined. In this way, a simple morning wake-up song becomes a call to inner vigilance: to seek the Lord’s grace through purity, right discipline, and the treasure of the Aṣṭākṣarī received from an Āchārya, so that the name of Hari can truly take root and rule the heart.
