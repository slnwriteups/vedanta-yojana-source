# Day 21: Ēṟṟa Kalaṅgaḷ

@verse 21

Āṇḍāl first hails Krishna as the son of Nandagopa, whose great, generous cows pour milk in such abundance that the vessels overflow even before they can be changed, “ēṟṟa kalaṅgaḷ edir poṅgi mīdaḷippa māṟṟādē pāl shoriyum vaḷḷal perum pashukkaḷ.” This signals not just material prosperity but a giving nature that never dries up. Nandagopa becomes the model of a lord whose household is inexhaustibly generous, and Krishna, as his son, is heir to this nature. Then Āṇḍāl turns to Krishna’s cosmic identity: “ūṟṟam uḍaiyāy periyāy ulaginil tōṟṟamāy ninṟa shuḍarē tuyil eḻāy” – O one rich in noble qualities, O great one in this universe, O radiant light who has appeared here in visible form, awaken. Krishna is both beyond reach and yet standing among them as a boy in Gokula; this tension between transcendence and sweet accessibility forms the devotional core of the verse.

The second half of the pāsuram turns to the gopīs themselves: “māṟṟār unakku vali tolaindun vāshal kaṇ āṟṟādu vandun aḍi paṇiyumā pōlē pōṟṟi yām vandōm pugaḻndu.” His enemies, the kings humbled by His valour, came to His door at last with nowhere else to go and fell at His feet. The gopīs too have come to His door, but not like them; they have been drawn by His qualities, His compassion and gentle accessibility, and they come to praise Him. Unlike Kākāsura, who wandered all three worlds without finding refuge before falling at Rāma’s feet, they come knowing who He is, singing at dawn as Periyāzhwār sang Tiruppallāṇḍu, with no doubt that their purpose will be fulfilled. The hidden purport, the commentators say, is that of a true disciple who comes to the feet of the āchārya and takes up his service, and to whom the āchārya then imparts all the essential truths.
