# Godā Rangamannar Kalyāṇam

## Mārgazhi, Kūḍāravalli and the Sacred Timeline

Godā Rangamannar Kalyāṇam marks the sacred culmination of the Mārgazhi month and is celebrated at the very end of the month, just before the Sun enters Makara at Makara Sankrānti (Pongal). In Sri Vaishṇava understanding, Mārgazhi is a period of vrata and spiritual discipline, centered on the daily recitation of Tiruppāvai. Within Mārgazhi, the 27th day, Kūḍāravalli, is crucial, as Āṇḍāl receives the Lord’s boon of marriage and formally concludes her Pāvai Nōnbu.

The actual Kalyāṇam is observed at the end of Dhanurmāsam, especially at Sri Rangam, where the Lord is worshipped as Ranganātha, known affectionately as Rangamannar, the royal bridegroom.

## Upbringing in Devotion and Resolve

Āṇḍāl’s unwavering dedication to Perumāl begins with her upbringing by Periyāzhwār, Vishṇuchittar, who raised her in an atmosphere of intense devotion and sacred knowledge. As young Kōdhai matured, her love for the divine crystallized into a singular resolve to marry only Perumāl himself, never to accept any earthly suitor. Her heart remained eternally fixed on Ranganāthar of Tiruvarangam, and this single-minded devotion finds poetic expression throughout Tiruppāvai. Through her sacred songs, she teaches that sharaṇāgati—complete surrender at the Lord’s feet—is life’s highest and ultimate goal, earning her a place in Tamizh tradition comparable to Rādhā in the North as the supreme exemplar of bhakti.

## Temple Traditions and Sacred Garlands

Temple tradition holds that Ranganātha accepted Āṇḍāl’s garlands with love, married her in a celestial union, and she merged with the deity to become His eternal consort. Her sacred garlands continue to play a vital role in Vaishṇava ritual life across multiple temples. Garlands worn by Āṇḍāl at Srivilliputtūr are sent annually to the temple of Venkateshvara at Tirumala for the grand Brahmotsavam, especially for the auspicious Garuḍa Sevā procession. In reciprocal devotion, Tirumala sends garlands back for Āṇḍāl’s marriage festival at Srivilliputtūr. Her garlands are also dispatched to Kallazhagar (Azhagar Kōyil) during the Chithirai Festival, connecting her worship across the sacred Vaishṇava pilgrimage sites.

## The Sacred Wedding and Divine Merger

After Āṇḍāl’s forty-day Mārgazhi vrata of Tiruppāvai, the Lord appeared in three sacred dreams: to her father Bhaṭṭarpirān, to the king of Sri Rangam, and to the temple priest. All received the same divine instruction to arrange Āṇḍāl’s marriage to Ranganātha at the Sri Rangam temple. On that blessed day, she was united with Sri Ranganātha in the inner sanctum. From that moment, she merged with the deity, transformed into an eternal form, worshipped as Āṇḍāl forever at His side. Thus the soul’s month-long vrata of surrender blossomed into divine union, and she became the eternal consort and intercessor for all who recite Tiruppāvai.
