# Day 20: Muppattu Mūvar

@verse 20

Āṇḍāḷ first hails Krishna as the one who goes before the thirty-three devas in battle, standing at their front to remove their fear and lift their burdens, “muppattu mūvar amararkku mun śenṟu kappam tavirkkum kaliyē.” The “thirty-three” is traditionally understood as the 8 Vasus, 12 Ādityas, 11 Rudras, and 2 Aśvins, symbolizing the whole deva-saṅgha that depends on Him for protection. Krishna is addressed as valorous and truth-filled, “śeppam uḍaiyāy tiṟal uḍaiyāy,” whose strength is not brute force but righteous power used to comfort His own and burn the enemies of His devotees. When Āṇḍāḷ calls Him Vimalan, spotless one, she highlights that His punishing of opponents and consoling of devotees are both completely free of selfish blemish. This double address—protector of devas and refuge of bhaktas—frames the gopīs’ call: “kaliyē tuyil eḻāy … vimalā tuyil eḻāy,” O remover of fear and O blemishless Lord, kindly awaken from sleep and turn that same protecting glance towards us.

Āṇḍāḷ then turns to Nappinnai, describing her with the language of Śrī: soft, full bosom, red lips, slender waist—“śeppanna men mulai ccevvāy cciṟu maruṅgul”—and directly addresses her as Tiruvē, clearly identifying her with Lakshmī. In Śrīvaiṣṇava understanding this makes Nappinnai the puruṣakāram, the compassionate consort who always joins the Lord in the work of protecting devotees. The gopīs therefore do not ask Krishna directly to come out for the bath; they ask Nappinnai, “ukkamum taṭṭoḷiyum tandun maṇāḷanai ippōdē emmai nīrāṭṭu,” meaning, “With the chamaram (fan) and the hand-mirror in your hand, hand over your Lord to us now and help bathe us.” The fan and mirror are royal emblems, so the request is not just to see Krishna but to receive Him in full royal, auspicious mode for their vratam. On the inner level, they are asking Śrī as Nappinnai to present Krishna to them in a way that completes their initiation—like a guru’s final bestowal of grace—so that this “holy bath” marks true entry into His service. In this way, pāsuram 20 closes the Nappinnai-triad by affirming: Krishna saves all, destroys harm to devotees, but it is through the smiling consent of Tiruvē–Nappinnai that the soul is actually led to His side.
