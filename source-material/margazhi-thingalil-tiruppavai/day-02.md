# Day 2: Vaiyattu Vāḻvīrgāḷ

@verse 2:1-4

Āṇḍāḷ begins with “vaiyattu vāḻvīrgāḷ,” addressing all who live in this world and reminding them that being called into this vrata is a privilege, not something automatic. When she adds “nāmum nam pāvaikku śeyyum kiriśaigaḷ kēḷīrō,” she is inviting her companions to pay attention to the specific observances they are about to take up for this pāvai-vratam. The heart of those observances is summed up in “pāṟkaḍaluḷ paiya ttuyinṟa paraman aḍi pāḍi”: their primary work is to sing of the feet of Paraman, the Lord who reclines on the milk-ocean in seeming rest while quietly sustaining all. “ney uṇṇōm pāl uṇṇōm nāṭkālē nīr āḍi” then shows how this inner focus shapes outer life; they set aside ghee and milk for the period of the vow and bathe in the early hours, allowing even ordinary habits like eating and daily schedule to realign themselves around the remembrance of his feet.

@verse 2:5-8

Āṇḍāḷ now moves from who is invited into the pāvai-vratam to how that call reshapes everyday life. She asks her companions to set aside kajal (añjanam) and flower garlands, not because beauty is suspect, but because, in this season, she wants them to wear devotion itself as their main ornament. Their rhythm also changes: they rise and bathe in the quiet early hours of Mārgazhi, when the devas are said to be awake, so that even their routine leans toward the Lord resting in Pāṟkaḍal.

From there she turns to character. The group quietly resolves to keep away from what they know is wrong and to guard their speech, refusing to let tongues meant for the Lord’s names become tools for gossip, complaint, or fault-finding. Finally, Āṇḍāḷ directs them toward generosity and compassion: sharing what they can, guiding others toward help when they themselves cannot give, and keeping in view what truly helps a jīva move toward uplift and freedom. In this way, the second half of the pāsuram shows that the pāvai-vratam is not just about giving things up, but about living simply, speaking gently, and acting kindly, until ordinary life itself begins to mirror their love for the Lord of Pāṟkaḍal.
