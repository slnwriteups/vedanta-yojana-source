# Day 3: Ōṅgi Ulagaḷanda

@verse 3:1-4

This pāsuram shows what happens when a place receives Krishna and begins to live in rhythm with his presence in Āyarpāḍi Gokulam. Āṇḍāḷ first directs the girls’ attention to the Lord they are actually singing of: the One who once measured all the worlds in three strides, the same Vāmana–Trivikrama adored in divya-deśams like Tirukkōvalūr, Śīrgāḻi, and Kanchipuram. She hails him as Uttaman, the “best” and “purest,” utterly distinct from achetana tattvams (insentient matter), from chetana tattvams (bound souls like us), from mukta-ātmas (the liberated), and even from the nityasūrīs who eternally serve him in Śrī Vaikuṇṭham, so that his very name itself stands out as the real treasure and security of their village.

@verse 3:5-8

From that Lord, grace then spills over into the land and daily life. When people honour him and live in accordance with their dharma, the seasons themselves fall into a right rhythm: hot summers followed by steady, cooling rains, and then a gentle winter that allows the land to rest so the farmers can reap good, healthy produce. The fields of Āyarpāḍi stand full and green; fish move through the flooded roots, and bees circle around the flowers and grain, so the whole landscape feels alive and well tended. Āṇḍāḷ finally points to the cows of the village: the calves drink first, and only the remaining milk is taken, so the cows yield freely and the pots fill quickly. For those who keep this pāsuram close and let it shape their priorities, the result is a very concrete kind of well-being—regular rain, healthy crops, generous cattle, and a village that knows its safety and prosperity rest in Krishna’s care rather than in its own strength.
