# Day 17: Ambaramē Taṇṇīrē

@verse 17

Āṇḍāḷ begins by honouring Nandagopan as the generous king who freely gives away clothes, water, and food to those in need: “ambaramē taṇṇīrē śōṟē aṟam śeyyum em perumān nandagōbālā eḻundirāy.” She calls him “our Lord,” acknowledging his protective role over the clan. Next comes Yaśodā, praised as the slender, radiant young mother, the brightest light among all gentlewomen: “kombanārkkellām koḻundē kula viḷakkē em perumāṭṭi yaśōdāy aṟivuṟāy.” Then the gopīs address Krishna, the cosmic measurer who pierced the sky and strode the three worlds with two steps: “ambaram ūḍaṟuttōṅgi ulagaḷanda umbar kōmānē uṟaṅgādeḻundirāy.” Finally, Baladeva, the golden-footed prince, is called to rise with his cherished younger brother: “śem poṟ kaḻal aḍi ccelvā paladēvā umbiyum nīyum uṟaṅgēlōr em pāvāy.” The sequence builds from earthly protectors to divine siblings, showing respectful progression through the household.

On the esoteric level, this pāsuram encodes the four essentials for bhakti-siddhi: seeking the āchārya, receiving the tirumantiram, understanding its meaning, and realizing its essence. Nandagopan represents the Āchārya, likened to Vishṇu who imparts the sacred mantra to worthy disciples, praised for his boundless generosity like the divine teacher. Yaśodā symbolizes Lakshmī, bearer of the tirumantiram itself, the virtuous consort who held Krishna in her womb, radiant as the mantra’s inner light. Krishna embodies the meaning of the mantra, the cosmic Lord whose līlās reveal its profound truths. Balarāma signifies the essence, inseparable from Krishna as Ādiśeṣa is from Nārāyaṇa, ensuring the complete realization. The gopīs’ plea for Balarāma to awaken with Krishna underscores holistic sādhana—mantra, artha, and sāra together. Thus Āṇḍāḷ teaches that union with God requires this sacred progression through guru, mantra, comprehension, and ultimate absorption, mirroring the family’s inseparable bond.
