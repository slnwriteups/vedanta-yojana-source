# Day 14: Uṅgaḷ Puḻaikkaḍai

@verse 14

In this pāsuram, Āṇḍāḷ stops pointing to distant signs like birds far away or conches in a remote temple and instead focuses on what is happening right inside her friend’s own backyard. She says, “Look at your pond and garden.” The red lotus has opened its mouth-like petals to greet the first rays of the rising sun, while the blue lily that blooms at night is now closing, signalling that dawn has surely arrived. Even if you doubt nature and say that climate and seasons have changed, there is another unmistakable sign. The ascetics, clad in ochre robes, their teeth gleaming white and their lives rooted in austerity, have already gone to their temples to blow the conch and open the sanctum. Some ācāryas see here a gentle contrast between false renunciates who only appear spiritual and true sādhus whose purity shows in their conduct, speech, and discipline. Within this very local, immediate scene, Āṇḍāḷ builds pressure on her friend. You promised yesterday that you would be the one to wake us all up and lead us, you even spoke at length about how reliable you are. Yet today you lie asleep while the world of flowers, saints, and temples is already standing in readiness for the Lord.

When Āṇḍāḷ reminds her of this broken promise, the girl inside feels ashamed and falls silent. Āṇḍāḷ then softens her tone and shifts from rebuke to honour, addressing her as “naṅgāy,” the blemishless one, and “nāvuḍaiyāy,” the one gifted with powerful speech and eloquence. You are someone whose words carry weight, whose tongue is meant for truth and praise, not empty talk. So rise without shyness and join us in singing the glory of that Lord who holds the conch and discus in his broad hand and whose eyes are like the lotus. The whole pāsuram quietly teaches that learning and ability, especially the gift of speech, must not be wasted in hypocrisy or mere argument but must be surrendered to correct understanding and devotion. Tradition salutes Śrī Rāmānuja as the true nāvuḍaiyān of this verse, the one whose speech, fragrant with all the śāstras, was used wholly to praise the Lord and to uplift the world. Āṇḍāḷ’s call here becomes a call to every seeker. Do not sleep in self-contradiction when your mind, speech, and learning were meant to awaken others. Use them to join the community of devotees and to praise the lotus-eyed Lord with clarity and sincerity.
