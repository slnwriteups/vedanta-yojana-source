# Day 11: Kaṟṟuk Kaṟavai

@verse 11

This pāsuram shows Āṇḍāl waking a friend who is the daughter of a powerful, flawless chieftain, someone rich in cattle, courage, and reputation.

She begins with the father: his herds are vast and well-kept, grouped into many kinds, and yet he is humane enough to let the calves drink their fill before any milk is taken. At the same time, when enemies or antisocial elements threaten, he does not hesitate to ride out and crush them in their own stronghold, just as Rāma marched to Laṅkā and defeated Rāvaṇa on his own soil. He is kuṟṟam onṟillāda kōvalan—a cowherd-lord without blemish in generosity or in valor—and this girl is his golden, creeper-slender daughter, beautiful and soft like a peahen moving among these fearless warriors.

Āṇḍāl points out that all the neighbourhood friends have already gathered in the courtyard to sing the names of the mugilvaṇṇan, the cloud-hued Lord, yet this wealthy girl lies still, neither stirring nor speaking. She teasingly suggests that perhaps growing up in such comfort, with such status, has wrapped her in a kind of heaviness and pride that keeps her from stepping out. Finally she asks the core question: "For what possible reason are you still asleep?" The whole pāsuram is a loving push to someone born into privilege and spotless lineage to rise above that comfort, come out of her jewelled home, and join the bhāgavata gōshṭi—the moving, singing company of devotees who are going house to house, praising Krishna together and walking toward Vishṇu as one.
