# Day 25: Orutti Maganāy

@verse 25

Āṇḍāl begins with the paradox of Krishna’s childhood: “orutti maganāy ppiṟandu ōr iravil orutti maganāy oḷittu vaḷara.” Krishna is born as the son of one mother (Devakī) and, in a single night, is carried away and raised as the son of another (Yashodā). This double maternity expresses how the Lord belongs both to the realm of vow and destiny (Devakī in prison) and to the realm of tender daily love (Yashodā in Gokulam). Āṇḍāl then recalls how Kaṃsa’s evil plotting could not touch Krishna; instead, his own wicked thought burned in his belly like fire: “tīṅgu ninainda karuttai ppiḻaippittu kkañjan vayiṟṟil neruppenna ninṟa neḍumālē.” The Lord’s mere existence turned Kaṃsa’s interior into a furnace. The teaching is simple yet sharp: in front of the Lord, hostile intent punishes itself; adharma becomes its own consuming fire.

From this remembrance of Krishna’s inscrutable ways, the gopīs speak their purpose plainly: “unnai aruttittu vandōm paṟai tarudi yāgil.” They admit they cannot fully grasp how He rewards good or punishes evil and therefore do not argue cases; they simply declare, “We have come to You, knowing You as refuge, and now we seek paṟai from You.” Here paṟai is moksha seen not as escape but as sanctified service. They promise, “tiruttakka shelvamum shēvagamum yām pāḍi” – we will sing of Your sacred, faultless wealth (divine qualities, līlās, Nappinnai’s grace) and of our own service at Your feet. In return, they ask that all sorrow and inner agitation be dissolved so that their remaining life is only praise and joy: “varuttamum tīrndu magiḻndēlōr em pāvāy.” The inner message is that worship of Krishna in His human form – as the child of two mothers, as the one who quietly undoes Kaṃsa – is not “less” than worship of His cosmic forms; it is the very path by which one enters a state where every song, every act of service, becomes participation in His eternal bliss.
