# Day 16: Nāyaganāy Ninṟa

@verse 16

Āṇḍāl speaks to the guardian of Nandagopan’s mansion, calling him, "O protector of the palace of Nandagopa, our leader; O guardian of the grand gateway where the festive flags and tōraṇas hang." She asks him to open the bejewelled door (maṇikkadavam), because the young cowherd girls (āyar shiṟumiyarōm) have come as a group, after Krishna had already given his word the previous day that he would grant them the paṟai – the blessing and the sacred drum that symbolizes the fruit of their vow. They say, "We have come pure, sleepless, with minds focused only on Him, to sing and awaken Maṇivaṇṇan, the blue-gem hued Lord of wondrous deeds." Since they stand there with such pure intent, they plead with the doorkeeper: do not at once reject us with a sharp "no"; instead, kindly open the door, moved by affection for us.

On the inner level, the gopīs are the devotees seeking entry not just into a physical palace but into Vaikuṇṭha, the Lord’s eternal abode. To enter, they must first be received by the gatekeepers, who represent the Āchāryas who stand at the threshold of divine grace. The palace of Nandagopa suggests the Lord’s abode, and the two types of guarding mentioned point to the two doorways a seeker crosses: entrance into the fold of the Āchārya, and entrance into the Lord’s own presence through the teachings. Interpreters link these two "guards" to the mūla-mantra and dvaya-mantra, taught by Nārāyaṇa to Lakshmī and passed down through the Āchārya-paramparā, which function as spiritual keys that open the way to moksha. The plea "do not refuse us immediately, we have come pure" becomes the disciple’s prayer to the Āchārya: kindly accept us, do not push us away at first sight, and bestow these mantras so that we may approach the Lord who has, through his many avatāras, already assured that those who seek his protection with a sincere heart will receive liberation. Thus this pāsuram shows that the path to Krishna is trodden by approaching the gate of grace – the compassionate teacher and the mantras – before entering the inner sanctum of divine union.
