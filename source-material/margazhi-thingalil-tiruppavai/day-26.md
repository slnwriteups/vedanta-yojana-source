# Day 26: Mālē Maṇivaṇṇā

@verse 26

Āṇḍāl addresses Krishna as Mālē Maṇivaṇṇā – the Lord of boundless love with the hue of a blue gem – and reminds Him, "we have come to bathe in Mārgazhi as our elders did." She makes it clear that this is not an innovation but walking in the footsteps of earlier generations, aligning their vow with the customs of their āchāryas and family elders. Then she lists what is needed: the Pāñcajanya-conch, white like milk, whose sound makes the whole world tremble; the big paṟai drum, fit for great celebrations; a group of those who will sing pallāṇḍu, auspicious benedictions; lamps to light the space; a flag to fly for Him; and a canopy like a great pavilion above. She is effectively saying: "Give us everything needed to do your pūjā properly; let our ‘bath’ be this full, traditional worship of You."

The inner teaching is that true Mārgazhi snānam is not mere water-contact but entering the stream of correct worship as handed down by elders. Blowing the conch corresponds to invoking the sacred om and announcing the Lord’s presence. The sounding of paṟai and other instruments stands for arousing joy and attention in the community of devotees. Decorating the Lord and singing mantras and shlokas are the offering of beauty and word. Lighting the lamps connects to offering naivedya and ārati, bringing both sustenance and light before Him. The flag belongs to His processions, proclaiming His sovereignty in the outer world, while the tent or canopy is the cover under which He rests, denoting intimacy and protection. Āṇḍāl thus shows that the gopīs’ "bath" is actually a sixfold worship-cycle, and what they seek from Krishna is the right to perform it, exactly as their elders did, so that their entire life becomes a daily immersion in His service, not just a single ritual dip.
