# Day 30

@chapter Vaṅgak Kaḍal Kaḍainda

@verse 30

In this last pāsuram, Āṇḍāl steps forward in her own voice. Through twenty-nine verses she has walked with the gopīs: setting the time and the vow, waking her friends house by house, rousing the doorkeepers, Nandagopa, Yashodā, Balarāma, Nappinnai and Krishna Himself, and at last receiving His assent to their prayer for unending service. Now she gathers it all up and declares its fruit: those who recite these thirty verses will receive the grace of the Lord and His consort, and will be filled with joy in this world and the next.

She begins with the Lord who churned the ocean, "vaṅgak kaḍal kaḍainda mādavanaik kēshavanai." When the milk-ocean was churned, He bore Mount Mandara on His back as the tortoise; the poison went to Shiva and the nectar to the devas, but He Himself took the hand of Sri, who rose from the waves, and so He is Mādhava, the Lord of Lakshmī. He is Keshava too, the Lord of the beautiful tresses, whose hair Āṇḍāl delights in as it tosses with the churning. The ocean He churned is also the ocean of samsāra, and He is the boat that carries across it all who take refuge at His feet. "Pray accept us as You accepted Her," is the gopīs’ unspoken prayer. To this Lord the moon-faced, jewelled girls of Āyarpāḍi went, bowed, and received their paṟai, "tiṅgaḷ tirumugattuc cēyiḻaiyār shenṟiṟaiñji aṅgap paṟai koṇḍavāṟṟai." The rains came to the village through the month, and the gopīs gained the greatest boon of all, communion with Krishna and the kingdom of His service.

Then Āṇḍāl names herself: Kōdhai, daughter of Bhaṭṭarpirān of beautiful Puduvai, Srivilliputtūr, who wore cool garlands of fresh lotus and offered them daily to the Lord of that town. Her song is a "shaṅgat tamiḻ mālai," a garland of the purest Tamizh, and its thirty verses are to be recited without omission, "muppadum tappāmē," for a necklace that loses a single gem loses its worth. Whoever recites them so, she promises, will be blessed everywhere by the four-shouldered, red-eyed, lotus-faced Tirumāl, never apart from Sri, and will rejoice: "eṅgum tiruvaruḷ peṟṟinbuṟuvar." Parāshara Bhaṭṭar taught that one should rise early and recite all thirty pāsurams; failing that, at least the twenty-ninth, "shiṟṟam shiṟugālē"; and failing even that, one should remember the joy of having once recited and heard them. The vow of the gopīs thus becomes, through Āṇḍāl’s grace, a vow that every one of us may keep.
