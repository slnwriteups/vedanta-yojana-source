Jyeshthābhishekam
@image 2

Every year, when the Jyeshthā nakshatram rises in the summer sky, the temple on the hills of Tirumala observes one of its most meaningful and intimate festivals, the Jyeshthābhishekam, also known as the Abhideyaka Abhishekam. It is celebrated over three days in the Kalyāṇa Maṇḍapam of the Sampangi Prākāram.

Unlike the grandeur of the Brahmotsavam, the Jyeshthābhishekam turns inward, to care, sanctity and responsibility. Its purpose is to preserve and protect the ancient utsava mūrtis of Sri Malayappa Swāmy, Sridevi and Bhūdevi, the very forms that bless millions of devotees through countless sevas and processions throughout the year.

The utsava mūrtis, believed to be over eight hundred years old, were discovered in a dense valley of the Seshāchalam Hills called Malayappa Kona. Over the centuries they have been lovingly worshipped, carried in processions and bathed in sacred abhishekams. To protect them from wear and to maintain their sanctity, the Vaikhānasa Āgama, in its Prakīrṇa Adhikāra, prescribes the Abhideyaka Abhishekam, a ritual that renews a spiritual and physical shield over the mūrtis.

On the first day, the golden kavacham, the armour the deities usually wear, is reverently removed. The mūrtis are anointed with Visesha Sugandha Tailam, a special herbal oil with medicinal and protective properties. Then follows the Ashtottara Shata Kalasha Snapana Tirumanjanam, in which one hundred and eight silver kalashas, filled with sacred dravyas such as milk, honey, coconut water, turmeric and sandalwood, are used in a grand abhishekam, to the recitation of the Pancha Sūktas and other Vedic chants.

In the evening the Lord is adorned with the Vajra Kavacham, an exquisite armour studded entirely with diamonds, and with His Consorts by His side, Sri Malayappa Swāmy is taken in procession around the four Māḍa streets, dazzling the devotees with this rare and radiant form.

On the second day the deities are adorned with the Muthangi, a delicate armour of pearls whose elegance offers a softer, more serene vision of the Lord. As on the first day, the evening brings a procession along the Māḍa streets, and devotees can witness this graceful form, which is seen only once a year, at the Jyeshthābhishekam.

@image 4

The third day is devoted to the sanctification and adornment of the Suvarṇa Kavacham, the golden armour worn by Sri Malayappa Swāmy and His Consorts throughout the year. Before it is placed on them, the armour is purified and consecrated afresh according to the Āgamas. Once adorned, the deities remain in this form through all the major sevas and festivals until the next Jyeshthābhishekam.

@image 5

Although the Suvarṇa Kavacham remains on the deities all year, abhishekams such as the Snapana Tirumanjanam are still performed during the important utsavams, the Brahmotsavam, the Vasantotsavam and the Pavitrotsavam, when the deities receive a full bath. Outside these occasions, the temple follows careful procedures that limit direct contact, so that the vigrahas are preserved for the long term without any loss to the richness of the worship.

References to sacred festivals at Tiruvenkaṭam, the ancient name of Tirumala, appear as early as the Sangam period, in Tamizh classics such as the Ahanānūru of the first century CE. Even two thousand years ago, then, pilgrims were climbing the hills to celebrate the Lord. Through every dynasty, administration and modern change, the essence of this ritual has stayed intact, thanks to the tireless care of the Vaikhānasa archakas, the Ekāngis and the Jeeyar Swāmy, under whose guidance the Lord is worshipped exactly as the Āgamas prescribe.

The Jyeshthābhishekam reminds us that divine love is expressed not only through offerings and praise but also through care, reverence and responsibility. In it the Lord blesses us with His beauty and His darshan, and also with the lesson that even the eternal form deserves protection and mindful service. May Sri Malayappa Swāmy bless us all in His many adorned forms, of diamond, pearl and gold, and may we continue to uphold the sacred traditions through which His grace flows from generation to generation.

Sri Malayappa Swāmy Tiruvaḍigaḷe Sharaṇam.
