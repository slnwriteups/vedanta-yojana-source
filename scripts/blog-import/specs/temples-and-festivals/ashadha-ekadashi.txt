Āshāḍha Ekādashi and Pandharpur
## Āshāḍha Ekādashi
Āshāḍha Shukla Ekādashi, observed in the bright fortnight of the month of Āni (Āshāḍha), is one of the most prominent Ekādashis of the Vaishnava calendar. Widely known as the Devashayanī Ekādashi or the Prathama Ekādashi, it marks the beginning of the Chāturmāsya, a four-month period given to spiritual discipline, inner reflection and a renewed commitment to dharma. In western India the day is also celebrated by the great Wārī, the pilgrimage to Pandharpur, where thousands of devotees gather for the darshan of Pāṇḍuranga Viṭṭhala, a form of Vishnu bound up with His devotee Puṇḍalīka. What follows looks at these traditions as a Sri Vaishnava sees them.

Ekādashi is the eleventh tithi of both the waxing and the waning fortnight. For most Vaishnavas it is a day of fasting and of more śravaṇam (hearing), smaraṇam (remembrance) and worship of the Lord. In Sri Vaishnavism most vratas, vows, are regarded as kāmya, undertaken for a particular material or spiritual end, and so they are not generally undertaken by those who have performed sharaṇāgati, or prapatti, the path of complete surrender. Ekādashi fasting is the exception. It is not undertaken for personal gain but as kainkaryam done at the Lord's command, and while it is not obligatory for prapannas, observing it is a valid expression of discipline and reverence, especially as part of service in a temple or of one's family tradition.

Āshāḍha Ekādashi is set apart because it opens the Chāturmāsya. It is called the Devashayanī Ekādashi, because on this day Vishnu is believed to enter yoga-nidrā, His cosmic sleep; the Prathama Ekādashi, the first of the Ekādashis of the Chāturmāsya; and the Mahā Ekādashi, because it sets the spiritual tone for the season ahead. In Sri Vaishnava temples the day may bring a special archanā, recitation of the Divya Prabandham, and an alankāram showing Śrīman Nārāyaṇa reclining on the Ocean of Milk, the Kshīra Sāgara.

## Chāturmāsya
Chāturmāsya means four months. It begins on Āshāḍha Shukla Ekādashi and ends on Kārttika Shukla Ekādashi, which is also called the Prabodhinī Ekādashi.

In the Sri Vaishnava understanding, it is a season in which Śrīman Nārāyaṇa, having accepted offerings through the first half of the year, enters His rest, yoga-nidrā. His devotees answer by entering a state of restraint, inner purification and deepened devotion, and the jīva is encouraged to withdraw from indulgence and turn its energies to the śāstras, to kainkaryam and to the remembrance of its surrender.

Though nothing in it is obligatory, the Chāturmāsya is commonly kept with dietary niyamas, giving up certain foods such as curd, salt or fried things; with alpa-bhojanam, eating less than usual to preserve sattva; with svādhyāya, the study of sacred texts such as the Bhagavad Gītā, the Divya Prabandham, the Śrī Bhāshya or the Rahasya Traya Sāram; and with kainkaryam in the temple or at home, helping with the archanā, with the cleaning or with the offering of naivedyam. For a prapanna these are not austerities that earn merit but expressions of loving service and remembrance.

## Pāṇḍuranga of Pandharpur
@image 3

Pandharpur lies on the banks of the Chandrabhāgā river in Maharashtra and is the principal centre of the worship of Viṭṭhala, who is held to be a manifestation of Krishna, of Vishnu. The temple is ancient, with architectural features that suggest Hoysala and Yādava contributions. It is not one of the hundred and eight Divya Desams, but many Vaishnava traditions honour it as an abhimāna kshetram, a place of deep experience of the Lord. The mūrti of Viṭṭhala is distinctive: He stands on a brick with His hands on His hips, facing the devotee directly, and this form is explained by the story of Puṇḍalīka, which remains central to the sanctity of the place.

@image 4

According to the traditional accounts, Puṇḍalīka was a householder who lived near the present Pandharpur. At first he cared little for dharma and neglected his parents, but in time he was transformed, some say after meeting pilgrims or sages, and he turned to a life of intense devotion to them. He served his aged parents with complete attention, treating them with reverence and seeing to their comfort at all times.

One day Lord Vishnu, pleased with this change in him, came to his home. Puṇḍalīka was attending to his parents at that moment and could not rise at once to welcome his divine guest. Instead he picked up a brick and asked the Lord to stand on it while he finished his service. Far from being displeased, the Lord stood patiently on the brick, moved by Puṇḍalīka's sincerity, and He remains there still, in the form we now know as Viṭṭhala, the Lord who waits for His devotee.

Seen through Sri Vaishnava eyes, the story shows that kainkaryam done with a pure intention is itself worship of the Lord, and that even the duties of a householder, when they are joined to surrender and service, lead to the experience of Bhagavān.

## The Wārī
Every year lakhs of devotees, the Wārkarīs, make the Wārī, a pilgrimage on foot to Pandharpur that culminates on Āshāḍha Ekādashi. The tradition goes back several centuries. It carries the pālkhīs of saints such as Sant Tukārām and Sant Dnyāneshwar, it rings with the singing of abhangs, Marathi devotional verses rich in personal bhakti, and it is centred on singing together, on satsang and on service to the community. Its forms of worship differ from those of the South Indian Vaishnavas, yet the spirit of the Wārī echoes themes dear to the Āzhwārs: devotion regardless of caste or social standing, spontaneous remembrance of the Lord in song, and a people moving together as a form of spiritual ascent.

A Sri Vaishnava may keep Āshāḍha Ekādashi in several ways: by upavāsam, fasting partly or wholly as health and strength allow; by śravaṇam and pāṭham, reciting the Divya Prabandham, the Vishnu Sahasranāmam or the Gītā; by offering flowers, naivedyam or garlands to the Lord at home or in a temple; and by formally entering the Chāturmāsya with a sankalpa of restraint, study or service. The point is not austerity for its own sake but to use this season to set one's life afresh in harmony with the Lord's purpose, without attachment to the fruits of one's actions.

Āshāḍha Ekādashi, the Prathama Ekādashi, opens a distinct season of the Vaishnava year, a season of inner renewal and spiritual discipline. Through the story of Puṇḍalīka, the practices of the Chāturmāsya and the great Wārī to Pandharpur, the day brings together personal devotion, the life of a community and the rhythm of the śāstras. For a Sri Vaishnava these traditions meet in one teaching: true devotion is not confined to ritual but is present just as fully in attentive, sincere service, in the temple, in the home and on the roads walked by millions.
